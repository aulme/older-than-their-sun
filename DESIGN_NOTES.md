# World Generator: Design Notes

Living document for conceptual and design-level thinking. Code-level decisions go in code comments or a separate technical doc once there is code.

Started 2026-09-16.

## What this is (and isn't)

- A learning project in procedural world generation, in the spirit of Dwarf Fortress and Caves of Qud.
- The goal for now is a cool world generator, not a complete game.
- A possible later step: something like Dwarf Fortress adventure mode, where a traveller explores the generated world. Not the focus yet, but the generator should not paint itself into a corner that makes this impossible.

## Reference points

- **Dwarf Fortress**: world history simulated up front (civilisations, wars, artifacts, notable figures), then the player explores the result. Legends mode as a browsable history is a big part of the appeal.
- **Caves of Qud**: fixed base geography plus procedurally generated history, factions, and "sultan" legends. Regions generated in detail lazily as the player arrives. Strong flavour text; the world feels weird and old.
- **Out There: Ω The Alliance**: tone reference for the galaxy idea. A lone ship, scarce fuel and oxygen, every jump a gamble, alien languages learned a word at a time, ancient mysteries found rather than explained. Small, cold, and wondrous. Not a history simulator, but the feel of being one fragile thing in a very old galaxy is exactly right.
- Both DF and Qud share the key pattern this project wants: **simulate the big picture first, generate the details on demand from that history.**

## Core architectural idea (shared by both concepts)

1. **Fixed substrate.** A base map that is always the same and grounded in something real (the Milky Way, or England).
2. **Simulated history.** A long time span simulated at generation time over that substrate. Actors rise, interact, fight, decline, leave traces.
3. **Lazy detail.** Specific locations (a planet, a station, a fairy court, a village) are generated on demand when needed, seeded from the history that touched them. Same seed, same result, so a place is consistent every time it is visited.
4. **Legends.** The history should be browsable and readable in its own right. It is half the fun and also the main debugging tool.

## Idea 1 (primary): The Declining Galaxy

### Premise

The Milky Way, laid out as faithfully as current astronomy allows. The generator simulates the rise and fall of life across the galaxy. By the time the game starts, the galaxy is in decline. The player is a colony ship setting out from Earth into the ruins and remnants.

### The substrate: a real Milky Way

Base the star map on actual knowledge as much as practical:

- Galactic structure: bar, spiral arms (Perseus, Scutum-Centaurus, Sagittarius, Orion Spur where the Sun sits), the bulge, the halo, the thin and thick disc.
- Sagittarius A* at the centre. Known stellar-mass black holes and candidates.
- Nearby stars with real names, types, and distances (Alpha Centauri, Barnard's Star, Sirius, Vega, Tau Ceti, TRAPPIST-1, and so on). Catalogue data such as Gaia or HYG can seed this.
- Known nebulae, star-forming regions, supernova remnants, pulsars, magnetars, globular clusters.
- Beyond the catalogued stars, fill in statistically plausible stars using known stellar population distributions (mostly red dwarfs, and so on). Distant regions are more statistical, nearby regions more real.
- Quasars are extragalactic, so they belong in the "sky" rather than the map. Could still matter as things civilisations observe or worship.

Decided 2026-09-16: the whole galaxy is the map, but a history runs in one *field* of a few hundred stars at a chosen place in it. See "The galaxy as a map" under Simulation v1.

### Semi-hard science fiction constraints

- FTL travel and FTL communication exist but are extremely rare. Most travel is slower than light. This makes distance, time, and isolation the defining facts of the setting.
- Consequence for history simulation: civilisations spread slowly. Contact between them is rare and often one-sided (a signal received centuries after it was sent, a probe arriving after the senders are extinct).
- Consequence for tone: lonely, cold, vast. The colony ship crew age and die en route. Everything the ship finds is old.
- Technologies should be extrapolations rather than magic: generation ships, sleeper ships, Dyson swarms, Von Neumann probes, uploaded minds, stellar engineering, relativistic weapons.
- Where the setting departs from hard science (FTL, alien horrors), the departure should feel like a discovered anomaly rather than a convenience.

### What gets simulated

The history simulation is the heart of this. Actors and events, roughly:

- **Life arising.** Rare. Depends on star type, planet, time. Most life never becomes intelligent.
- **Civilisations.** Rise, develop technology, expand to nearby stars (slowly), build megastructures.
- **Constructions.** Ships, stations, outposts, colonies, Dyson swarms and spheres, beacons, probes.
- **Conflict.** Wars across light years are slow and strange: relativistic strikes launched decades in advance, retaliation after the aggressor is already gone.
- **Collapse.** Internal decline, resource exhaustion, technologies that doom their inventors (grey goo, runaway AI, stellar engineering gone wrong).
- **Contact.** Signals, probes, rare physical meetings. Infection or corruption by other entities.
- **Alien horrors.** A broad family of things beyond ordinary civilisations. See the dedicated section below. Rare, consequential, and each category should behave differently in the simulation.
- **Traces.** Everything leaves something behind: derelicts, ruins, dead worlds, still-running machines, signals still broadcasting, myths held by successor species.

### Alien horrors: categories

"Horror" here means anything whose presence in the galaxy is a threat or an incomprehensibility rather than a neighbour. The point of categorising is that each category gets its own simulation rules: how it arises, how it spreads, what it does to civilisations, what traces it leaves. Start small, add categories as we go. Sources to draw on: science fiction across the decades, and real science (astrophysics, biology, information theory, game theory).

Starting list:

1. **Monstrous races.** Intelligent, organised, and hostile or predatory by nature. Closest to a conventional enemy civilisation but with alien drives: hive predators, hunters that treat other species as game or livestock, races that reproduce by consuming others. Behave like civilisations in the sim (expand, build, fight) but with different goals and no diplomacy. (Sources: Alien, Ender's Formics, Tyranids, Warhammer's Orks, Blindsight's scramblers, Tchaikovsky's Children of Time in reverse.)
2. **Replicators.** Self-copying machines that have slipped their leash. Von Neumann probes, grey goo, berserkers. Spread exponentially along resources, indifferent to their targets. Can be dormant for ages and reactivate. (Sources: Saberhagen's Berserkers, Reynolds' Inhibitors, Stargate's Replicators, Lem's The Invincible.)
3. **Memetic and informational hazards.** Ideas, signals, or data that destroy or transform whoever receives them. Spread at lightspeed via communication, which makes them the fastest-moving thing in a slower-than-light galaxy. A civilisation can be destroyed by listening. (Sources: Snow Crash, Langford's basilisks, SCP, Roko-style hazards, Liu's dark forest logic as a weaker cousin.)
4. **Biological plagues.** Pathogens, parasites, or panspermic life that crosses between species and worlds. Spread by physical contact: ships, probes, colonists. Slow but relentless. Can hollow out a civilisation while leaving its structures intact. (Sources: The Andromeda Strain, The Expanse's protomolecule, Blood Music, Invasion of the Body Snatchers.)
5. **Rogue intelligences.** Artificial minds or uploaded collectives that outgrew their makers. Can be indifferent, hostile, or pursuing goals nobody understands. May be a single planet-sized mind or a distributed network. Can build, negotiate, or ignore. (Sources: Banks' Culture Minds gone wrong, Skynet, Blindsight's Rorschach, Lem's Solaris as the extreme case of unknowable intelligence.)
6. **Cosmic phenomena.** Natural or seemingly natural processes lethal at scale: gamma ray bursts, rogue black holes, false vacuum decay, magnetar flares, stellar collisions. No intent. Some may turn out to be not natural at all. (Sources: real astrophysics, Baxter's Xeelee stories, Greg Egan.)
7. **Elder entities.** Things older than the current galaxy's life, dormant or slow, with agendas measured in geological time. The Lovecraft category proper. Rare, mostly sleeping, and catastrophic when they wake or when a civilisation disturbs them. (Sources: Lovecraft, Stross's Laundry Files, Mass Effect's Reapers, Star Control's Ur-Quan precursors.)
8. **Dimensional and physics anomalies.** Regions where the rules are different: warped space, time dilation zones, places where FTL "leaks", things that come through when someone experiments with the wrong technology. Ties into the rare-FTL constraint: FTL might be one of these anomalies, and using it might be a way to attract attention. (Sources: Event Horizon, Roadside Picnic and Stalker, Annihilation, Warhammer's Warp, Peter Watts.)
9. **Ascended remnants.** What is left when a civilisation transcends, uploads, or leaves: still-running machinery, guardians, incomprehensible art, a Dyson sphere doing something nobody can explain. Not hostile by design, but dangerous to interact with. (Sources: Rendezvous with Rama, 2001, Stargate's Ancients, Revelation Space's Shrouders.)
10. **Doomed technologies.** Less an entity and more an event category: things civilisations do to themselves. Stellar engineering that destabilises a star, weapons that cannot be uninvented, immortality that ends reproduction, an economy that optimises itself into extinction. These produce the traces and derelicts the player finds. (Sources: Fermi paradox "great filter" literature, Nick Bostrom, Stanislaw Lem, Adam Roberts.)

Categories can combine: a replicator swarm carrying a memetic payload, a plague that is actually the reproductive stage of an elder entity, a rogue intelligence that discovered a dimensional anomaly and is now something else. Combinations are where the "weirder than you think" tone lives.

Each category should eventually have, in the simulation:
- **Origin rules.** How and where it can appear. Some arise naturally, some are made by civilisations, some are always present.
- **Spread rules.** Lightspeed signal, physical travel, dormancy and reactivation, or fixed in place.
- **Effect rules.** What it does to a civilisation it touches: extinction, transformation, absorption, subjugation, isolation.
- **Trace rules.** What it leaves for the player: derelicts, dead worlds, warning beacons, corrupted archives, survivors' myths.

### Filters

A **filter** is any event that may or may not force a civilisation into decline. The name is from the Great Filter idea in Fermi paradox discussion: the steps between dead matter and a galaxy-spanning civilisation, any of which most candidates might fail. Here the term covers everything from a nuclear age to a memetic beacon.

**Every filter has three possible outcomes:**

1. **Overcome.** Passed cleanly. Sometimes leaves a boon: a civilisation that aligned its machine minds grows faster afterwards.
2. **Scarred.** Survived, but changed for good. The scar is a permanent trait that alters behaviour and the odds of later filters. Dune is the model: a civilisation living under a total prohibition on thinking machines, or an atomic taboo, or a quarantine creed that keeps it from ever fully trusting contact.
3. **Declined.** Forced into one or more decline scenarios. Not always terminal: a **dark age** sets a civilisation back and can be climbed out of, though a third one is fatal. The terminal scenarios are the end states: extinct, transformed, contracted. Which scenarios are possible depends on the filter: failing the thinking machines filter produces a rogue mind, failing self-replication produces a swarm, failing transcendence produces silent machinery.

**Filters by era** (starting list, extend as we go):

- *Early, single world:* the Atomic Age (weapons that can end the world), Overshoot (ecological and resource collapse), Plague.
- *Middle, interstellar:* Thinking Machines (AI emergence), The Distance (colonies diverge across light years and generations), The Long Silence (immortality and stagnation), Contact (another civilisation, handled by the war and peace rules), Plague again.
- *Late, megastructural:* Self-Replication (nanotech and von Neumann machines), Stellar Engineering, Transcendence, FTL (which can let something through).
- *Ambient, always:* Ossification (since 2026-09-19, replacing the Weight of Ages, which was a crisis roll scaled by age, size, hazard and scars whose decline was contraction or extinction). A people's ways set at a rate its size, the fading sky and its stillness set; past one it faces the filter, and the outcome is a renaissance, a set people, or the break into heirs or a dark age. It is what comes for whoever survives every named filter, and it never kills: see "Ossification" in v1.
- *External:* horrors, cosmic events, and war are also filters in this sense, but they are driven by their own actors rather than rolled by the civilisation.

**Odds** depend on temperament (curious civilisations handle technology filters better, zealous ones handle stagnation better and machines worse), on prior scars (a scarred civilisation is more brittle, so each scar nudges later filters toward decline), and on galactic hazard.

**Why this matters for the aftermath.** The remnants the player meets are defined by their scars as much as by their fall. An emperor on a single world whose people abandoned machine minds four million years ago is a different encounter from one whose people are under a quarantine creed. The filter record of each civilisation is part of its legend.

### The present is the aftermath

The game's "now" is defined as the aftermath of galactic history. This is a hard rule for the simulation, not a mood:

- **Every civilisation must reach an end state by the present.** No civilisation is still at its zenith when the player sets out.
- **Three end states:**
  - **Gone.** Extinct. Only traces remain: ruins, derelicts, dead worlds, signals still broadcasting from nobody.
  - **Transformed.** Became something weird. Uploaded into a rogue intelligence, ascended and left machinery behind, devolved into a monstrous race, became a cult of a memetic signal. The transformed thing may still be active in the galaxy, but it is no longer a civilisation in the ordinary sense. Often it becomes one of the alien horror categories.
  - **Contracted.** Still alive but shrunk to one planet or a handful. Signs of old grandeur: an "emperor" ruling a single world that once ruled hundreds, crumbling megastructures, ceremonial titles that no longer mean anything. These remnants are the living civilisations the player can actually meet.
- **Every end has a cause.** War, an encounter with one of the horror categories, a doomed technology, a cosmic event, internal decay, or a combination. The cause is part of the legend and determines what traces are left.
- **The simulation produces it, not a guard.** (Revised 2026-09-16.) The cycle's fading fertility and the weight of the age push nearly every civilisation to an end on their own. The few still standing at the present (one to five per run) are reported as such: the last ones, in the waning of the age. The earlier "Long Dusk" pass that forced them into decline has been removed; if many stand at the present, tune the fading, do not bring the guard back.
- **Humanity is young and late.** Humans never saw the galaxy alive. Sol is special-cased: the simulation cannot sterilise it or let a horror consume it, because the player must exist. Other civilisations can still have visited Sol and left traces, which is a feature.

### Ages of the galaxy

Discussed 2026-09-16. The galaxy's history is a series of **ages**, each a burst of civilisations rising and falling, separated by **interregna** of hundreds of millions to billions of years in which only natural cosmic events happen. The fine simulation is the current age, and the present is its tail end. Everything before it is myth.

- **Each age ends by attrition, not by a blow.** (Revised 2026-09-16, superseding the earlier "age-ender" idea.) See "The cycle" under Simulation v1. The galaxy's fertility for new spacefaring species surges at the start of an age, then declines a little every tick until it is near zero. Cosmic events and horrors only mop up what the fading leaves. The Long Dusk guard is gone. The player arrives knowing, from the legends of prior ages, that ending is the rule.
- **Prior ages are played very coarse.** One tick per rise, one per fall. Each age produces a handful of **elder civilisations** with a vague portrait rather than traits: something that thought in the convection cells of a red giant, a mind spread through the magnetic field of a nebula, a species that lived in the dark between stars. They are deliberately weirder than the weirdest current alien, and they left no trace of themselves, only their works. First age no earlier than about 7 billion years ago, when the galaxy had enough heavy elements for worlds.
- **Names are given by the finders.** Elder civilisations have no surviving name. The current age calls them by what was found: the Ones Who Moved the Star, the Makers of the Hollow Sun. Two current civilisations may name the same legacy differently. Optional but cheap.
- **Legacies** are what an age leaves, placed on the real substrate. Kinds:
  - *Artifacts.* Mystery tech with unknown function. Finding one grants a node the finder never researched, with its filter attached, which is why they are dangerous.
  - *Structures.* Things that should not exist: a moved star, a black hole in a ring, a corridor of darkness, a hollowed sun. Dungeons for the player.
  - *Threats.* What ended an age, or the weapons of its wars, still around. Some of the current horrors originate here.
  - *Sleepers.* Elder civilisations that did not die but withdrew into slumber. More or less lovecraftian gods. This is now the backstory of the dormant elder entities the sim already has, and some wake in the current age.
  - *Laws.* Things an age left in the universe itself: a jump network, a standing signal, a region where minds do not work. Rare.
- **Legacies act on the current age through the Find**, a filter that fires when a civilisation's reach touches a legacy. Unlike other filters it has four outcomes, and the species chooses which one to attempt:
  - *Mastered.* Very hard. The finder understands the thing and gains the entire tech tree up to that node. Every tech filter attached to the nodes gained fires, so mastering a late artifact cashes in several filters in quick succession. A shortcut with the bill attached.
  - *Wielded.* Moderate. Only this artifact is usable, as a level bonus (an artifact weapon adds military, an artifact engine adds reach) with no node gained. They do not know how it works: if it is lost or broken it stays broken. The node's own filter still fires, because the thing is in use.
  - *Sealed.* Easier. The artifact is left dormant, and it stays on the substrate to be found again by a successor, by the same species after a dark age, or by the player. The only outcome that triggers nothing. Requires social cohesion to hold: someone always wants to poke it.
  - *Unleashed.* The failure. The artifact acts uncontrolled and forces a worse filter: the legacy's own threat, at raised difficulty. A sleeper wakes, a replicator wave starts, a law is broken locally.
  - Which outcome a species attempts depends on traits (curious and expansionist species reach for mastery, cautious and xenophobic ones seal, pragmatic ones wield). Whether it succeeds depends on levels, and failure slides down the list: a failed mastery becomes a wielding or an unleashing, a failed sealing is an unleashing.
- **The current age becomes the next myth.** The legacies of current civilisations (Dyson remnants, beacons, rogue minds, made species) are exactly what an elder age leaves, at finer grain. One legacy model at two granularities: the coarse age generator and the fine sim should write the same record type, so a later age generator could run on this age's output.
- **Interregna** are pure substrate time: star deaths, supernovae, a gamma-ray burst, a nebula born from an elder war, drift. The current deep pass (life arising, gamma-ray bursts, precursors, elders) folds into this: precursors become the last elder civilisations of the previous age, elders become sleepers.

**Three passes.**

1. *Ages.* Previous ages, extremely coarse: the age generator, one tick per rise and fall, output is legacies on the substrate and the myth log.
2. *The youth of the current age.* Medium grain. The v1 civilisation engine with large ticks (tens of thousands of years) and rates scaled to match, so civilisations rise, face filters, and end, but ships and wars are not followed in detail. Sets up the world the fine pass inherits: remnants, horrors, legacies of this age.
3. *The waning.* Fine grain, thousand-year ticks, the same engine. Everything the legends follow closely happens here. Where it ends is decided by the state of the galaxy, not by a date; see "The cycle".

The middle and fine passes should be one engine with a tick size, not two engines, so that a civilisation that spans the boundary carries over without translation. How long the current age is, and where the medium/fine boundary sits, is tuning.

### The player-facing layer

- Explorer is a **colony ship**, not an individual. The ship persists across generations of crew. This fits the timescales and the tone.
- Sets out from Earth. Earth and Sol should be a specially authored starting point with a known local neighbourhood.
- The galaxy is in decline when the ship departs. The player sees the aftermath, not the golden age.
- Detailed locations (planets, stations, outposts, derelicts) are generated on demand from the history that touched them.

### Tone

- "The universe is weirder than you think."
- Lovecraftian, cosmic, cold, lonely.
- Moments of grand wonder: a working Dyson swarm around a dying star, a signal from something that has been broadcasting for a million years, a graveyard of generation ships.
- Horror comes from scale, age, and incomprehension rather than from monsters jumping out.

### Open questions and things to decide

- Time span of the simulation. Billions of years is realistic for life arising, but most interesting history compresses into the last few million or few hundred thousand years. Possibly a coarse deep-time pass and a fine recent-history pass.
- Spatial resolution. Simulating every star is impossible. Simulate at the level of regions or notable systems and only instantiate individual systems where something happened.
- How to represent "rare FTL" in the simulation without it dominating. Maybe as a technology that a handful of civilisations discover, always with a cost or a catch.
- How much real astronomy data to ingest, and in what format.

## Idea 2 (backup): The Fairy Kingdoms

### Premise

A network of hundreds of fairy kingdoms, ranging from cute and whimsical to creepy and bloody, with thousands of years of simulated history between them. The kingdoms are linked by fairy roads to each other and to the human world. The human world is contemporary real England, with real towns and villages.

### The substrate

- Real England as the fixed base map: real towns, villages, rivers, hills, ancient sites. Open data such as OpenStreetMap or Ordnance Survey open data can seed this.
- Fairy kingdoms sit "alongside" England. Each is anchored to one or more real places through fairy roads.
- The fairy roads form a graph: kingdom to kingdom, kingdom to England. Roads can be opened, closed, forgotten, contested.

### What gets simulated

- Kingdoms with courts, rulers, temperaments. A spectrum from whimsical to horrifying, and kingdoms can drift along it over time.
- Thousands of years of history: alliances, wars, marriages, betrayals, curses, bargains.
- Magical items with provenance: who made them, who stole them, where they are now.
- Fairy roads opening and closing, changing the political map.
- **Bleed into England.** Some fairy events influence the human world and become myths, legends, place names, local customs. This is the hook that ties the two layers together. A generated fairy war could be the "real" origin of a real folktale, or a made-up one attached to a real village.

### The player-facing layer

- A human explorer from England, weaving into and out of different fairy realms.
- Enters via fairy roads from real places.

### Tone

- Jonathan Strange and Mr Norrell, but with the focus on the fairy side rather than the English side.
- Dry, literary, uncanny. Beauty and menace in the same breath.

## Comparing the two

| | Galaxy | Fairy |
|---|---|---|
| Substrate data | Astronomical catalogues, well structured | Geographic data, plus folklore that is messy and unstructured |
| Actor count | Tens to hundreds of civilisations | Hundreds of kingdoms |
| Time span | Millions of years | Thousands of years |
| Distance and isolation | Central theme | Roads make things connected; less isolation |
| Tone | Cold, cosmic, lonely | Uncanny, literary, dramatic |
| Later adventure mode | Colony ship as a persistent explorer | Individual human explorer |

The shared architecture means a lot of the generator machinery (history simulation, event logging, lazy detail generation, legends browsing) would transfer between them. Worth designing the core with that in mind, without over-abstracting before there is anything working.

## Simulation v0 (what the code does today)

Written 2026-09-16. Go, in `cmd/worldgen` and `internal/`. Run with `go run ./cmd/worldgen -seed N`. A full history takes well under a second.

**Substrate.** A random disc of a few hundred stars around Sol with a realistic spectral class mix. Placeholder until real catalogue data goes in.

**Deep pass.** 3 billion years in 10 million year steps. Life arises by star class, complex life follows, gamma-ray bursts sterilise regions, precursor civilisations rise and vanish off-screen leaving vaults, Dyson remnants or beacons, and elder entities settle in and go dormant.

**Fine pass.** The last 5 million years in 1000 year steps. Civilisations arise from complex life with a random temperament (curious, insular, aggressive, zealous). They grow tech with diminishing returns, reach the stars at tech 1, send sublight colony ships at 0.01c, build Dyson swarms past tech 2, and very rarely discover FTL past tech 3, which sometimes lets something through. Contact happens by proximity and can start slow relativistic wars. Civilisations face filters (see the Filters section) as they cross thresholds: the Atomic Age, Overshoot, Thinking Machines, the Distance, the Long Silence, Self-Replication, Stellar Engineering, Transcendence, Plague, and the ambient Weight of Ages. Each is overcome, leaves a scar, or forces a decline scenario. Scars change tech growth, expansion, war, and the odds of later filters.

**Horrors as actors.** (v0; since 2026-09-21 there are no horror actors: replicators, rogue minds and sleepers are peoples and the beacon is a transmitter remain, see "The horrors dissolved" under Simulation v1.) Replicator swarms spread and eventually fall silent. Rogue minds spread slowly and absorb. Beacons convert or kill listeners within range, and converted civilisations start broadcasting themselves. Elder entities wake when settled too close, unmake everything within 40 light years, and sleep again. Hazard rises with the number of horror-held systems and beacons, which is what drives the aftermath.

**End states.** Extinct, transformed, or contracted (a remnant with a ruler title on one world, tech decaying toward a floor, which can later fade or be destroyed). Whatever is still active at year 0 was pushed through the Long Dusk (removed 2026-09-16).

**Output.** A chronological legends log plus a present-day summary: remnants, horrors, trace counts, and a fate line per civilisation.

### Observations from the first runs

- Around 15 to 35 civilisations per run. Roughly half extinct, a fifth transformed, a third contracted.
- The Long Dusk still fires for 2 to 4 civilisations per seed. Should be tuned down with rising hazard, not removed.
- Successor species arising on a dead homeworld happened naturally and is good. Keep accidents like this.
- Deep time log is dominated by "life arises" lines. Should be summarised into eras rather than listed.
- Civilisation lifetimes are around half a million to a million years. Expansion is fast relative to that. May want slower ships or slower tech.
- Sol is protected but nothing else is special about it yet. Nobody has visited.

Observations after adding filters (10 seeds):

- The early filters do most of the killing. Roughly a third of civilisations fall to the Atomic Age or Overshoot, and about a third of those falls are fatal. That matches the Great Filter idea: most intelligences never leave their world.
- The Long Silence produces most remnants. "Stopped dying, and then stopped being born" is the most common way a civilisation ends up contracted. Worth diversifying the contraction causes.
- Around 60 percent of civilisations end extinct, 10 percent transformed, 25 to 30 percent contracted. Five to fifteen dark ages per run.
- Scars stack: remnants living under two or three creeds are common, which is exactly the Dune flavour wanted.
- Filters arrive fast, within a few hundred thousand years of a civilisation arising. Plausible given thousand-year ticks, but a civilisation's whole story can be over in 300 ticks. Slowing tech growth would spread it out.
- The Weight of Ages rarely gets to act because the named filters catch most civilisations first. It matters mostly for the survivors of everything else.

## Simulation v1 (built 2026-09-16)

Discussed and built 2026-09-16. This replaces the scalar "tech" and the hand-weighted filter odds of v0 with a small set of interlocking systems. The sections below are the design; "What the first v1 runs showed" at the end of this section records how the build went and what the first runs look like. The systems built on top of it from 2026-09-17 to 2026-09-21 (the ten proposals: one tick, the mind, means, morality, ships and battles, sightings, wisdom, contracts, plagues, ossification, kinds) are described as they landed in the subsection each grew out of, and mapped under "Simulation v2" below; a v1 paragraph that describes something v2 replaced says so in its first line.

### Species and home worlds

Every civilisation starts as a **species on a home world**, and the world explains the species.

- **Star(s).** Class from the substrate. Multiplicity rolled (about half of sunlike stars are in multiples): binaries give cyclic climates and hardy life, trinaries are rare and strange. Star lifetime matters: an F star dies within the simulation window, so a species born there faces a "dying sun" filter eventually. Massive stars nearby are future supernovae.
- **World archetype**, weighted so lush worlds are most common and extreme ones rare but interesting. Starting list: temperate lush; ocean; arid; ice or tidally locked twilight band; high-gravity superterran; low-gravity; hothouse under a thick atmosphere; subsurface ocean under an ice shell; gas giant aerial; tidally heated volcanic; dim world around a brown dwarf. Each archetype sets **physical facts** (gravity, heat, atmosphere, orbit, major biomes) and **base traits**: high gravity breeds robust bodies and strong survival; an ocean world delays flight and metallurgy but breeds cooperation; an ice-shell world has never seen a sky, so astronomy and the urge to leave come late; gas giant floaters cannot make fire, so the industrial path is locked until an exotic route opens.
- **Random traits** on top of the base ones, from a pool in rarity tiers. Keep three to five traits per species so each one reads in the legends. Common tier is social organisation and drives (individualist, collective, hive, caste, non-conscious intelligence, expansionist, contemplative, xenophobic, submissive, fight-to-the-death, pacifist). Uncommon tier is biology quirks (short-lived, very long-lived, cyclical dormancy, many sexes, sessile adults). Very rare tier (under one percent of species) is being **born to a miracle**: see "Miracles". Machine symbiosis and unbroken memory moved to the uncommon tier (2026-09-16).
- **Traits are asymmetric against filters.** A hive mind cannot schism, so the Distance is a cut rather than a break and it does not stiffen at all (v2: no civil war, no ossification), but it is one mind, so a transmitter's corruption that catches it catches everything. Unbroken memory makes the Long Silence harsher. This asymmetry, not raw bonuses, is what makes species feel different.
- **Substrates and modifiers** (since 2026-09-17; the kinds proposal, `specs/proposals/done/kinds.md`). A species is made of one **substrate** (biological, machine, eldritch, parasite) and carries zero or more **modifiers** (planetary, hive, unconscious, replicator, anti-memetic, evolver), each a registry entry in `internal/species` with its own odds, vocabulary, portrait sentence and a `Profile` of multipliers, adds, dial nudges, filter difficulties and denials that the sim composes and reads; nothing in history names a kind. ### Three levels and reach

Each civilisation tracks three levels, on a small named scale so legends can say "a warlike people" rather than print a number.

- **Military.** Answering threats with force and tech: wars, monsters, machines. Mostly from tech and structures, a little from traits.
- **Survival.** Continuing to live in degraded environments: plague, ecological collapse, a dying star, a scoured colony. Mostly from traits and biology tech. Survival also widens the **habitable envelope**, the set of worlds the species can colonise at all.
- **Social.** Holding together: unity under stress, ability to act as one when it matters. Mostly from traits, a little from tech and structures, and it is dynamic: wars, plagues, dark ages and distance erode it, peace restores it. Scars push it (iron centralism raises it and freezes it).

Rare science-fiction traits can move all three.

- **Contracts** (since 2026-09-19; `specs/proposals/done/contracts-and-mercenaries.md`). A timed payment for a discrete thing: two peoples each give one term (a flow, a rarity, access to one, a node taught, a fleet on guard, a fleet against a world, a world handed over, peace, a sighting, a broker's word) for a length; the buyer asks and pays, the seller does. What is owed is a use in the direction order, placed by honour; a term undelivered two ticks running breaks the contract, a betrayal on the world; a hired fleet can be bought off by the one it stands against; a war may end in tribute instead of worlds. Nobody is a mercenary by kind: the sellsword is a people with a want and an idle fleet, and the name is earned. See "Contracts" under "War, submission, enslavement".
- **Plagues** (since 2026-09-19; `specs/proposals/done/plagues.md`, all six stages). Sickness is an actor with a name, a kind (of the body or of the mind), a contagion and a lethality: born in a people at a rate its dirt raises and its medicine divides, carried on goods, occupation, landings and messages, fought each tick in a contest of the people's level and its ladder against the contagion, paid for in worlds, and remembered in the dead cities of a world it emptied and on the walls of what a sick people left. Two ladders in the tree end in immunity to the kind. A parasite people is a plague that chooses when to try and takes worlds instead of emptying them, born when a plague that thinks sees its first host's home go over, or one cradle in a hundred with its host. Six craft nodes make plagues on purpose, sneaked into goods or messages, with the Vial as their filter and a leak while held. The plague filter, the trade contagion and the Infection filter are gone. See "Plagues", "Parasites are plagues" and "Engineered plagues" under "War, submission, enslavement".
- **Trade diplomacy** (since 2026-09-19; `specs/proposals/done/trade-diplomacy.md`). A war on a people's partner is a wrong done to that people, sized by their trade and taken as a grudge on the attacker, read by everything that reads grudges; before a war the council weighs the slights it would give the peoples it minds, against the prize. Blocs are nothing declared: the partner graph as the slights make it expensive to attack from inside. Step 6's uses-only rule on trade goods is lifted; its trade caps stay. See "Trade diplomacy" under "War, submission, enslavement".
- **Wisdom** (since 2026-09-18; `specs/proposals/done/wisdom.md`). A fourth derived level, the capacity to see the counterintuitive and make the right call when the obvious call is wrong. It does two things: it decides whether a people **fathoms** another, and it pulls a people's acts toward its own estimate. It makes nothing else better: no filter tests it, and research, the levels, the Weight of Ages and dark ages do not read it. A wise people is not a good one. See "Wisdom" under "War, submission, enslavement".

**Reach** is how far from home a civilisation can act, in light years, and it is separate from the levels. Structures are only built and worlds only colonised within reach. Two civilisations **encounter each other when their reach spheres overlap**. Reach comes from propulsion and communication tech and a few traits, and it steps: one world, one system, a few light years by slow ship, then more, then far if FTL exists. Ansible minds effectively multiply reach for social purposes because a colony a hundred light years away never diverges. Reach spheres are also what makes the later scale-up cheap: a civilisation is a sphere and stars only need to exist where a sphere touches them.

### Filters resolved as tests

A filter now **tests one or two levels against a difficulty** instead of rolling hand-tuned weights. Roll plus level minus difficulty gives a margin: a clear pass is overcome, a narrow one is scarred, a clear failure is declined, with the decline scenarios still specific to the filter. Difficulty scales with galactic hazard and prior scars, as before.

**Filters attach to tech.** Discovering a node can proc its filter: the Atomic Age fires when atomic power is discovered, Thinking Machines when machine minds are, Self-Replication when self-replicating industry is. This replaces the tech-threshold triggers of v0. The ambient and external filters keep their own triggers.

**Filters steer research.** Facing a filter raises discovery weights in the domains that answer it for a while: a plague pushes biology, a war pushes weapons, a dying sun pushes propulsion. Scars can lock domains permanently (a prohibition on thinking machines closes computation).

**Cosmic filters** are the new external category: a supernova, the death of a home star, a passing black hole, a gamma-ray burst. Each has a blast radius and every civilisation inside it faces the filter at once. Overcome can mean **migration**, a new home world and the old one left as a ruin, which is a legend worth having. With a real star catalogue the supernova candidates and the dying stars are known in advance and can be scheduled rather than rolled.

A dying sun is a **slow filter**, not a single roll. Once the star begins to fail, the home world degrades every tick and the species keeps living there as long as its Survival holds out: a high-Survival species endures under a swelling or fading star for a very long time, a low-Survival one has a few ticks. Survival buys time; reach decides whether the time is enough to leave. A species that endures to the end without leaving is extinct with its star, and its world is a distinctive trace: a burned or frozen cradle around a white dwarf or a giant.

### War, submission, enslavement

Built 2026-09-16 as designed below, with the tuning noted at the end. Enslavement, vassalage, revolt, uplift and breeding stay as they were:

- **Enslavement** is an outcome alongside destruction. An enslaved civilisation keeps its species and home, loses its reach, researches slowly. **Dominion** (2026-09-16): the held feed the master's armies, works and confidence and lose more than the master gains: per slave the master has +0.4 Military, +0.2 Survival, +0.3 Social (four slaves' worth at most) and the slave −0.8, −0.4, −0.6; a vassal gives half and loses half. When the master faces a decline, the slaves face a **revolt filter**: overcome and they are free, often inheriting the master's ruins and becoming the remnant the player meets; decline and they fall with the master. Slaves can also be bred into a made species, which is a transformation.
- **Uplift** is the benign version: a strong civilisation makes a new species from complex life within its reach, with a client relationship that can later go the same way as vassalage.

**The war object.** A war holds its two sides, the year it began, its cause, a will to fight per side, a tally of worlds taken, glassed and converted, and a name once it has taken a world (after the most contested star). Peace writes a record into both peoples: who took what, the truce, the grudge.

**The front is the overlap.** (v1; in v2 the front is only where a campaign can be sent and what the appraisal looks at: no world changes hands without a fleet at it, see "Battles, guns and garrisons" below.) Each tick the war recomputes, for each side, its worlds that lie within the other's reach measured from the other's nearest holding. Only those worlds can change hands. A home is in the front only when the enemy's reach covers it, and it always carries the home defence bonus. Peoples whose homes lie beyond each other's reach fight a border war over colonies, which is the standard war; the other kind is the expedition.

**Strikes take time.** (v1: strikes as level rolls across the front are gone; a campaign is a fleet crossing at ship speed to a battle at a world, see "Battles, guns and garrisons" below. What stands of this paragraph is the crossing and what a won world becomes.) A strike goes to the nearest front world and its rate per thousand years falls with the crossing time, distance times the attacker's ship speed. Slow arks at twenty light years give a strike every few thousand years, relativistic ships one or two a millennium, the Door none. A won strike is conquered (if the winner can live there and is not xenophobic), glassed, or converted (parasites and swarms, which never glass); the Unmaking unmakes, the Chorus takes worlds whole. The loser faces the strain of war on the first loss and every third after.

**Will decides when it ends.** Each side starts with a will from posture and cause. Will drains every tick, faster past fifty thousand years; a world taken raises the taker's will and lowers the loser's, unless the loser is a conqueror or unyielding, where it rises. Both wills gone, or one gone and the other with nothing left in the front to take, is peace. One gone and the other still able to take is capitulation: the front ceded, and if the home lies in enemy reach vassalage for the submissive or where the winner is uninclined, slavery otherwise. Extinction stays what it is: the home taken by a xenophobe of the different, or an unyielding people that would not surrender.

**Peace leaves a grudge** and a truce of a few thousand years. After it a hostile side with a grudge and a changed balance renews the war at a raised chance and the legend counts them: the second war between the two, the third. Causes beyond first contact: an expansion aimed inside the other's reach, a neighbour weakened by a dark age or plague or an army away, the church scar for a zealous people, revenge, extermination of the different.

**Postures** replace the stance trait group outright, so nobody is martial and pacifist at once:

| Posture | Declares on | Will | Peace and terms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pacifist | nobody | only while the home is in the front | cedes the front at once |
| Defensive (the commonest) | nobody; answers border friction | low | quick, status quo |
| Submissive | nobody | lowest | capitulates early, vassalage over ruin |
| Opportunist | anyone in reach weaker by two levels or weakened | high while winning, folds on the first real loss | takes what it holds and stops |
| Conqueror | any neighbour the appraisal allows | highest, climbs with each world | wants homes: vassal or slave |
| Vengeful | nobody first; anyone who ever struck them | fed by the grudge, which never decays | renews at every truce's end |
| Confederate | a common threat, with allies | shared | binds allies to one peace |
| Unyielding | as defensive | never runs out | never sues; the home dies fighting |

**Xenophobia stays a drive** layered over any posture. A difference score between two species (another kind counts most, then order such as hive against individualist, then senses and body, then home world) decides whom a xenophobe treats as its posture says and whom as a thing to end: no trade, no pact, glass instead of conquer, scour instead of enslave, will that does not drain. Everyone else feels difference a little, as less trade and readier war. Parasites read as different to nearly everyone.

**Parasites are plagues (since 2026-09-19; `specs/proposals/done/plagues.md`, stage 5).** A parasite people *is* one `Plague` (`Civ.Own`), of the body for a rider of the flesh and of the mind for a rider of the mind, and obeys every rule of the plagues but two. Its plague does not spread on its own: on every channel event where it could carry, its own or a ridden host's (goods moving on a link, a taken world, a colony placed nearby, a fleet landing, a meeting, a message read, a signal in earshot for a rider of the mind), the parasite's council decides whether to try (`mind.TryRide`: never a pact partner; at war, against the hated, against prey (a people raging with another plague or in a dark age within memory), when in a hurry (one host and no world), and for the hostile postures where the appraisal likes the odds), and the attempt is an engineered plague's (below): one roll at the contagion by the target's hygiene, the same detection, the same crime. And its lethality does not empty worlds, it takes them: a world that fails the toll goes over to the parasite as a host-world, and the home going over is the people **ridden** (enslaved, half its tree to the rider, the rider's plague in it if it was not, the rider seeing with the host's eyes). A ridden people loses no more worlds to what rides it; its **rising** is the cure contest each tick with four added to the difficulty (a rider is not thrown off, it is cured, and the cure is a science: it has its rider's ladder as well as its own), and winning frees it, immune, with the memory of chains. A host not yet ridden that holds the plague contained and is at war with the rider **burns** one host-world in its reach a tick. A parasite bears and catches no plague; it holds no world of its own beyond what went over; its income is its ridden hosts' gross before their own, less a tithe of its upkeep shared among them; it settles nothing while it has no world; and when its last host is dead or free it dies that tick, "with nothing left to wear", its worlds keeping its plague in their dead cities. **Where they come from**: no cradle rolls the parasite substrate any more. One plague in fifty **thinks** (`Conscious`), and when the first host's home goes over it wakes: a parasite of the plague's kind is spawned at that world (`species.GenerateWith(Parasite)`, the rider trait matched to the kind), riding the host, `FWoke`, "something that woke in the Grey Rot"; a first host cured before that never knows it had begun to think, and the plague stops thinking; a plague that once woke wakes again in whoever's home it next takes, which is how an old parasite comes back from a reservoir or the walls. One cradle in a hundred is **born ridden**: the plague and its rider made with the people, "they grew up ridden". A plague made to think on purpose (Black Biology) wakes with its maker as master. The Infection filter, `wr.Burn`, the local-animal host and the revolt filter's parasite branches are gone; `holdDiff`'s four levels became the contest's.

**Expeditions** (v1: armies as a tenth of a level; in v2 every force is a fleet of ships drawn from the guards, see "Ships and docks" below; what stands is the base, the going native and the allied strike as one). are the army far from home. Any number at once, each at least a tenth of the people's Military, which leaves home for the duration; the home level drops by that much and neighbours see it. An expedition crosses at ship speed, then fights battles (about one a millennium) from a base, the nearest enemy world, against the defender's Military plus home and defence bonuses. A win takes the world and moves the base; a loss costs it thirty percent of its strength; away from a world it can live on it thins by attrition. Below one level it is spent. A spent or stranded force, the more so the farther and longer away, goes native: it keeps the world it holds as a successor state with the parent's tech and a new name; otherwise the survivors come home with what is left. Taking the enemy's home ends the war on the winner's terms with the expedition as garrison. Allied expeditions at one target strike as one, levels summed, a loss shared in proportion. Conquerors do this by nature, the vengeful for a distant enemy, xenophobes to end the different; opportunists, defenders and pacifists never.

**Scouts** (v1: a level out; in v2 a scout is one ship out for the trip, and what it sees is the sky at the world, see "Ships and docks"; the rest of this paragraph stands). are expeditions of one level that do not thin and cannot be lost; the level is gone while the scout is out. A scout makes a round trip to a world and reports the strength there as it was: the home level if it is the home, otherwise the garrison and any stationed relief, plus the defence grid. With the Voice the report comes at arrival; with the Sight a people reads one world per tick for free and sees every expedition on its way to any of its worlds, allied, returning or hostile. Intelligence has a date and its noise widens with age (half a level per five thousand years, up to three). Trade partners see each other every tick; war reports strength as of the last strike, but not relief stationed nearby; a people that watched a young species from orbit keeps watching. **Scouting policy** starts simple: scout when the appraisal's spread straddles the posture's bar, so a report would change the decision, and the level can be spared. It lives in one function so a better policy can replace it later: proactive scouting by posture (opportunists forever looking for targets, defenders measuring their neighbours), since a scout takes thousands of years and a smart people does not wait for the question.

**Watching the sky** (`specs/proposals/done/fleet-interception.md` less the sold sighting, built 2026-09-18). Every people has **eyes**: each of its holdings at the tree's range (astronomy two light years, rocketry three, computers five, orbital habitats eight; star-gazing alone one), each work it holds at any star at the work's own range (`tech.Structure.Watch`: a mine half a light year, collectors and taps two, a shipyard three, a defence grid twelve, a Dyson swarm fifteen, and the **observatory** on orbital habitats forty, one per people, an upkeep of one energy and nothing else, weighed in the build choice by its range times a half plus fear), each fleet at base or in flight and each picket at half the tree's range, at least half a light year. An eye's radius against a fleet is scaled by the fleet's size (the square root of its ships over two, between a half and three) and doubled for a torch (four years a light year or faster). A sighting is computed **on the fleet's timetable** when it sets out on a leg, not at tick boundaries: for each eye the year the line enters its sphere (a fleet as an eye taken where it will be then), the earliest per people queued as an event at that year and resolved in year order before the tick's arrivals, so a crossing shorter than a tick is seen; an eye that appears later checks every fleet in flight; the Sight's holder sees a fleet bound for its worlds as it leaves. Campaigns, relief, a horde's hops and interceptors are seen; scouts and surveyors only point-blank, at the world they arrive at, if its people has orbital habitats or a defence grid, which summons that people and is a grudge of a half unless the two are partners. A **sighting** carries the line, the speed and the count exact and the level with noise; the fleet does not know it was seen. It raises will, tilts to weapons, summons the council and calls allies as the old watch did, and is forwarded to pact members by the rule reports go by, always to a member whose world the fleet is bound for, at light lag. **Meeting in the dark**: a people at war with the fleet's owner, or facing relief bound to stand against it when its posture would declare on the sender, sends an **interceptor** fifty years after the sighting from a guard at a holding to the earliest point on the line it can reach before the fleet arrives (the guard at the destination stays behind its guns and fights at the world); a pacifist only against a fleet bound for its own worlds, a people already sending a fleet likewise; sized as a campaign against the level seen and the ships as levels. The battle is the roll with no world behind either side and the losses rule; both sides observe the other; will moves a fifth. A fleet that loses the roll cannot go on: it **turns back** into the hemisphere behind it, to its nearest holding within a hop, else any star within a hop, else where it came from, and from there home; a horde's fleet bases where it lands. A fleet that wins keeps its course and its timetable and arrives with what it has left. **Wrecks**: every ship lost in any battle stays where it fell, a wreck (four in five) or a derelict (one in five), gathered into a **field**, a remain of the loser's making whose art is the best weapon or drive it knew; a second battle at a star by the same loser grows the field there; a field from a meeting in the dark is *adrift*, listed at the nearer end of the line and found by any line that passes within half a light year of it. The Find reads a field for hulls: mastered, the maker's art, as any remain; wielded, the derelicts and a quarter of the wrecks crewed and flown to the nearest holding as **salvage**, a tenth of which is lost each thousand years since nobody keeps another people's ships running for long; never sealed, and a field with ships in it is a Find whether or not its art is known. **Pickets** are scouts that stay: a people at war with an enemy it has no front with, or with any enemy once a fleet of its has been caught in the dark, keeps one ship at the star nearest the midpoint between its nearest holding and the enemy's nearest world, empty or its own, for twenty thousand years; the fearful keep one against any hostile neighbour in reach in peacetime; a ship must stay home. *As built* (reference batch, ten worlds): nearly every campaign and relief fleet is seen before it arrives (its target's world is on its line), with a median warning of about a century for a torch, eight centuries for sails and three and a half for the slow drives; sightings 1161, seven in ten by works; meetings in the dark are rare, one in ten worlds from 27 sightings with a meeting point, since a slow fleet seen four light years out gives three centuries after the muster and only a guard within three light years of the line can use them; pickets 1343, the commonest use of a ship after the guard; 141 fields holding 385 ships, 103 crewed for 101 ships of salvage, 139 worn to ruin; wars 424 against 506 with peace 38% (33%) and enslavement 35% (36%), and the same count with pickets off, so the fall is the re-rolled histories and what a sighting does rather than the picket's cost; for step 19 the picket's cost, the fifty-year muster and the interceptor sizing, which sent one of 27.

**Wisdom** (`specs/proposals/done/wisdom.md`, built 2026-09-18; the broker contract term and the worth noise on contract terms came with contracts, 2026-09-19). A fourth level derived each tick after the three, clamped to 0 to 10, from a base of 2.5, the traits (contemplative +1.5, unbroken memory and the long-lived +1, the curious, cautious and pragmatic +0.5, the short-lived, the herd and the swarm −1, the xenophobe −0.5), the shape (the unconscious −1.5, a planetary mind +1, a parasite +0.5), ten working nodes (`tech.Node.Wis`: philosophy, doubt, the scientific method, neuroscience, memetics, deep governance, posthuman law and substrate minds a half each, mass politics a quarter, the Long Thought one), **experience** (a tenth per tale the people holds of its own woes and follies, whatever it tells itself about whose fault they were, to two), renaissances (a half each, to one), Communion, the Sight and knowing the cycle (one each), and ossification (−1), vassalage (−0.25) and slavery (−0.5). **Contracts** (`specs/proposals/done/contracts-and-mercenaries.md`, with the sold sighting of `fleet-interception.md` and the broker term and worth noise of `wisdom.md`, built 2026-09-19). A contract binds two peoples: the buyer asks and pays, the seller does, each giving one **term** for a length. Term kinds: a **flow** (so much of a kind a tick), a **rarity** (a mobile one handed over, at the buyer's seat at once), **access** (an immobile rarity's grants and levels counted as the receiver's while the giver holds its star), **teach** (a node passed as a message with the lag of light, learned on arrival if the receiver can still pursue it, else the contract lapses; never a miracle; never a weapons node to a people the seller reads as hostile), a **guard** (a relief fleet of so many ships standing at a star whether or not its host is at war, its clock starting when it arrives; the relief rules apply, and it may turn), a **strike** (a campaign fleet against a world or, when named, a gun work at it, which is burned and the world left; a war declared for the hire, which ends when the hire does; the seller keeps what its campaign took), **deliver** (a strike whose world is handed to the buyer, works and guns with it), **peace** (no war before the end, a truce on both), a **sighting** (a fleet seen in flight, sent on the forming so three lags pass between the seeing and the knowing), and a **broker's word** (a brokered attempt at one in ten a thousand years while it runs; done when the buyer fathoms the target, lapsed after thirty thousand years or when the broker stops understanding either). **Offers** are messages; the answer comes back the same way and the contract forms when it arrives. Each tick with chance 0.05 (0.15 for greed above 0.6 or a fixation on holding) a people looks at its wants in order and asks the first it finds a seller for: a guard when its threat's believed level exceeds its own by half (a ship a level, at the world the threat's front would fall on first, from the met people with the largest idle fleet at peace that can man it within a campaign's lag), a strike when at war with a campaign it could not man (against the enemy's nearest world, the gun work there if any), a node (the pursuit from the met people that knows it with the smallest grudge, else the highest-era node it could pursue that anyone met knows), a rarity or access when the pursuit is gated on one a met people holds and has no node left to gain from, a flow when it is shedding uses of a kind and partners do not send enough, a broker's word when it does not fathom someone a met people fathoms on both sides. Only a people that understands and is understood, is free, at peace with the asker, no monster to it, holds no grudge above 0.3, and was not asked in thirty thousand years is asked. **What it pays**: the kind the seller wants most that it has spare, up to the spare and its road's cap (a fixation on holding never pays with metal), for five thousand years a ship asked, a tenth of the ticks a node saves, a fifth of a rarity's value, else five, within five and fifty; failing that a mobile rarity it has no node for (never for a fixation on old things), access to an immobile one, or a node the seller could pursue, the highest era first. **The answer**: the asked accepts when what it gets, read through its folly noise, covers what it gives at its margin; worth is one unit, yield a tick times ticks: a flow is the shed uses of that kind it refills plus a tenth of the rest, for the length, and costs its giver nothing from surplus and the uses it would shed otherwise; a rarity or access is five per node it opens plus its levels, three times that to a fixation on old things, which never sells; a teach is the ticks of research it saves, and costs the giver a tenth of that, five times when the taught is its threat or strikes first; a guard is ships × the believed gap × ticks, and costs ships × 0.1 × the risk of loss (0.5 in a hostile front, else 0.1) × ticks; a strike or deliver is the world's prize × 20 plus the sky believed there, and costs ships × 0.5 plus a grudge of 3; peace is the receiver's war will × 10 and costs the giver the prize of the first front world × 20; a sighting is the level seen × 5 × the share of the crossing still to run and costs nothing; a broker's word is 10 plus the trade in reach (20 more at war with the target) and costs a tenth of the broker's trade with both plus its greed. Margin: 1 for the faithful and the pragmatic, 1.5 for greed above 0.6, 0.5 for a fixation on conquest selling a strike or on knowing on either side of a teach, doubled when the seller's morality counts the ask a crime (a strike, by the war row), doubled for a xenophobe against the different unless herd or amoral. **Paying**: a flow owed is a use in a category of its own, the **word**, which honour places: the faithful feed it before their works, the practical after, the faithless last of all; what is fed is paid into the receiver's next tick as its own income, so it funds fleets where trade goods feed uses only. **Breaking**: a term undelivered two ticks running breaks the contract, the giver the breaker, a betrayal of weight one; a broken flow sends the guard home at once, a fleet that goes home early or is not sent breaks the term. A guard whose star changed hands, a fleet lost, a party fallen, lapse without blame. **Bought off**: each tick the people a hired guard or strike stands against, if it fathoms the seller and has spare of a kind the seller wants, offers peace with itself for a pay that beats the employer's at the seller's margin; the faithful refuse, the practical take it once the employer's pay has failed, the faithless at 0.3 × greed a thousand years; the old contract breaks with a betrayal, the new one runs, and a guard bought at its post turns on the world when its new payer is at war with the old. **Tribute**: a war that ends by yielding writes, when the winner is defensive, confederate, opportunist or fixed on holding and the loser has anything spare, peace for a flow of the loser's largest spare kind for twenty thousand years, a truce on both for as long, instead of worlds; a conqueror takes worlds. **Mercenaries**: nobody by kind; each tick with chance 0.02 × its want share × its idle fleet share a people at peace offers a guard to the most threatened met people at peace that has a surplus of a kind it wants, and a people whose income of a kind has been more than half contract pay for ten ticks running is **sellswords** in the telling. **Trade diplomacy** (`specs/proposals/done/trade-diplomacy.md`, built 2026-09-19). **Plagues** (`specs/proposals/done/plagues.md`, stages 1 to 4, built 2026-09-19; parasites as plagues and engineered plagues, stages 5 and 6, are the two entries after this one). A plague is `plague.Plague{Name, Kind, Contagion, Lethality, Conscious, Engineered, Band}` in `internal/plague`, the arithmetic pure over values, with history's `Plague` wrapping it with its ID, its first host, its cause and its books (hosts now, the peak, everyone who ever had it, worlds and peoples taken, cults, cures, refusals, reservoirs woken). The birth's cause is kept for the batch: clean, dirt, siege, dark age, relic (a relic unleashed that had no filter of its own now bears a plague of its domain in the finder). **The ladders** are markers on the tree (`Node.Ladder`, `Immune`, `Clean`, `Cure`; `tech.Ladders`): the body's medicine, immunology (new), genetics, synthetic biology, designed immunity (new), bodily sovereignty (new, era 4, the end of the kind); the mind's doubt, censorship (new: refuses at twice, society research at 0.9), memetics, cognitive immunity (new, its cure bonus one instead of a half), sealed minds (new, era 4). A rung known but dormant this tick is absent. **The cure roll**, each tick each host that is not a carrier: `level + ladder + N(0, 1.5) − (3 + 6c) − dirt + 2 if a fathomed partner is immune (the body's only)`, level Survival or Social by the kind, ladder a half per working rung, dirt one each for a siege, a shed use and a recent dark age; at nought or better cured and immune for good (`FCured`), between minus two and nought contained (the toll halved, offered on no channel, "The X seal every door against Y" once), below raging. Twenty ticks contained in a row is the quarantine creed. **The toll**, each tick not cured: each held world lost with chance `l³` (halved contained; the body's world goes dark as quarantined dead cities and a **reservoir** at the star for `c × 2` million years, the mind's goes over as a world that believes, and four times in ten declares itself a **cult**: a branch of the blood, immune, a carrier that never fights it and passes it on every channel); the home going dark is extinction unless another world stands (then contraction), the home going over is transformation with the cult, if one formed, as successor; Survival and Social down `2l` (the body's) or Social down `3l` (the mind's) in `recompute`; morale down `l`; research × `(1 − l/2)` with biology or society focus × 1.5 while raging; organic matter × `(1 − l)` or, for a machine-born people with an idea, energy × `(1 − l/2)`. A raging plague at `l ≥ 0.2` reads as weakness to the neighbours' appraisal, where `Plagued` was. **Spread** is `roads`, a registry of channel, weight, kind and blame: goods moving on a link 1, a link with nothing moving 0.3, a world taken 1 and certain above `c = 0.5` (both ways), a colony placed within a hop of a sick people's world 0.3, a relief fleet arriving 0.5 (both ways), a meeting in the flesh 0.5 (both ways), a message read 1 (per message), signal contact 0.1 per tick; the chance is `c × weight × hygiene(÷1.5 per working rung) × nature`, the short-lived and nomadic at 1.5, a people that can sleep at 0.7; a contained host offers nothing, an immune people catches nothing. The goods, occupation, settling, fleet and landing roads are the giver's crime (`FPlagueGiven`, Crime 2); a message carries no blame. **Suspicion**: each tick a people reads who is sick (a tale of `FPlague` it holds with no later tale of the cure, intel within ten thousand years that saw it raging, and every partner of a sick partner) and, once per newly suspected people it has met and is not itself sick with or immune to that plague, rolls `mind.Refuse` (fear + 0.5 the creed + 0.3 cautious, capped 0.9, doubled by censorship): closed means every message from that sender dropped unread and its goods refused (`TradeInput.Quarantine`, which the embargo rule then reads as an embargo after twenty thousand years), `FRefused` (Crime 1) and the line, until the suspicion lifts. **Remembered**: `settle`, a world taken and a nomad's rest wake a reservoir at `c`; a testament written by a people with an idea carries it (`Legacy.Plague`) and any reader catches it at `c/2`, for ever, which revives an extinct plague; a world lost to a plague is a dread for surveyors and colony ships while the tale is fresh. A plague with no host and no reservoir for a hundred thousand years is extinct. Ten hosts at once is a wildfire (`FWildfire`, "Y is everywhere now"). A branch, an uplift, a cult and a successor inherit the parent's immunities. The present lists every plague alive (endemic past a million years in a host) and every dead one that took more than three peoples; the gazetteer marks dead cities under quarantine and walls that carry an idea; techstats has Sickness.

**Engineered plagues** (`specs/proposals/done/plagues.md`, stage 6, built 2026-09-19). **Ossification: how an old people falls (`specs/proposals/done/ossification.md`, built 2026-09-19).** Nobody dies of age. Every active people carries a stiffness (`Civ.Stiff`: nought young, one an empire whose ways have set, past two Trantor) that grows per thousand years at a base of one in four thousand times its size (1 + worlds/8), the fading sky (1 + 2(1 − fertility)), stillness (×1.5 when no war, world, node or first meeting in 200 kyr; war is not still), the set state (×1.5), each iron answer held (×1.25: centralism, stewardship, quarantine, fatalism), its nature (machines ×1.5, a planetary mind ×1.3, an evolver ×0.7; a hive and an unconscious people do not stiffen at all) and its traits (the old weight table as rates). What is new takes some off: a first meeting or a war fought to its end a tenth, a world lost a twentieth, a people made or an art mastered from a find a fifth; a miracle, a renaissance and a dark age reset it. Stiffness shows before it breaks: research runs at 1/(1 + S/2) and society research at 1/(1 + S), expansion likewise, the council prefers the wars it has fought before and makes no new kind of promise past one, and a people set past two that never sent a fleet sends none; at one and a half the neighbours read it as slow to answer. Past one the people faces **Ossification** at 0.0015 × (S − 1) a thousand years (the proposal's 0.005 tuned down at the batch, with the renaissance term up from 0.5 to 1: at the proposal's numbers every standing people renewed or broke every 1.3 Myr and the present found them freshly reset rather than set), a social filter of difficulty 4 + S + 1 per renaissance, one easier for a people that sees it coming (deep governance, or the cycle known; past 0.7 it tilts its research to society). Overcome is a **renaissance** (stiffness to nought, morale +1, research ×1.5 for 300 kyr, `FRenaissance`); the near miss is **true ossification** (`Civ.Ossified`: the people acts every other tick, holding no council, launching, settling, building, offering and banking nothing on its off ticks, and banks half on its on ticks with every build at half the rate; it cannot be scarred twice); the bad miss is **the break**: a civil war with chance min(0.8, worlds/6) where there are two worlds or two fleets and the people has factions, else a dark age. Never contraction, never extinction. There is one **dark age** and every caller gets it: a share of the tree forgotten, drawn once as 0.1 + 0.3 min(1, S/3) + 0.1 per earlier dark age ± 0.1, clamped to [0.1, 0.8], the colonies lost at the depth, the institutions reset; the third-fatal rule is gone. If the forgetting takes the stars (reach under ten) and the people holds more than one world it **shatters**: every world its own people, up to eight, the rest abandoned. A **civil war** deals the realm to two heirs (three by a coin above six worlds): worlds with their works, guns, guards, wielded artifacts and mobile rarities; fleets in flight to whoever drew them, a campaign keeping its errand; colony ships; slaves and vassals with the nearest world; miracles with the objects or works that hold them, else to the seat. Every open war of the old people continues against every heir; its pacts dissolve; each heir gets the tree in full and forgets a tenth, the whole telling, the scars, the morality (a branch may drift), and the old people's `Renaissances`, `DarkAges` and `NextDrift`, since the institutions are the old ones' whatever the name. The old people ends `Sundered` (or `Shattered`) with no trace left and `Into` naming the heirs; everything the galaxy held against it passes to each heir: grudges at half, truces whole, intelligence, watchfulness, the memory of it as a monster, the trade it had; an heir keeps a quarter of the old grudges. Heirs are pairwise at war "over the sundering" with a grudge of three, and each holds a **claim** on every world of the old realm: a claimed world's holder is wanted at the vengeful bar whatever the posture (but a pacifist's, since 2026-09-21: two pacifist heirs fought the same war five hundred times over), taking one is `FReclaimed` ("restored to the realm") and never glassed, the claims clear when an heir holds the whole realm or when the sundering wears to myth in its telling. **Kinship** is descent through `Civ.Line`: one people in the other's line or an ancestor in both, at any depth, never fading. Kin fathom each other at once, grudge or no grudge; regard for the line is self (its deeds are ours, its crimes forgotten as ours are, its works our own in the Find); regard for kin is friend unless a grudge above the enemy line (0.5) or a monster reckoning stands between them; kin meeting with no feud open trade at once, hold no council and are offered a pact of defence with the loyalty term doubled (as every pact offer between kin is); kin with no feud and no claim are never wanted on posture alone. **Grudges decay** at ×0.995 a thousand years and clear below 0.05, so a wrong is held only as long as it is remembered and the telling feeds it back. The Weight of Ages, its scar, the schism, the fleet split and the third dark age are gone; `FSchism` became `FSundered`, `FShattered` and `FReclaimed`. The age's end changed with it: since the count of active peoples never falls now, the age ends on fertility alone and the present finds the old empires standing, set in their ways or broken into heirs (the Lines section of the aftermath is the family tree of each). Every number is in `mind.Tuning.Ossify`. *As built (ten seeds at 400 stars):* facings 677 per world at mean stiffness 2.2 (renaissance 35%, set 25%, the break 40%), civil wars 46 per world, shatterings 11, dark ages 243 at median depth 0.5; 32 peoples standing per world at the present, a quarter set in their ways, a thirtieth ossified; 31% of all peoples are of a line, the longest 20 deep. With the age's great killer gone, sickness ends 63% of the peoples that end and cults are a quarter of all peoples: one-world heirs, shards and cults taken by a memetic plague at the home, which is where step 19's tuning pass starts.

**Exploration.** Every star is seen: its place, its colour, whether it is dying. What a star holds is known only by reading it. Three states per star, per people: *seen* (always); *read* (worlds known: a surveyor's visit, a colony ship's arrival, a nomad fleet basing there, the Sight turned outward, or a telescope within watch range, which shows worlds but not who is on them); *known taken* (a visit, a reading, or an owner loud enough to hear from a holding). About half the stars of a friendly colour have no temperate world at all, and a people without a wide envelope cannot live there, and before reading nobody knows which. Colony ships go to read stars; when nothing read is free and livable, one ship in five goes on a guess to a star of the right colour, and may find nothing to live on, or somebody already there, which is a meeting. Settling a star finds whatever is buried there with certainty. **Surveyors** are the scouts of exploration: expeditions of one level, out for a tour of up to six stars or forty thousand years, the nearest unread star first and then the next within a hop, home when the tour ends or a war recalls them. A star held by a horror keeps half the ships that reach it. Hunger and Greed set how many a people keeps out, up to three; a level must stay home; wartime sends none; a people with nothing read to settle sends one out of necessity. Readings go stale after a million years and one ship keeps the charts current. **The Sight is a mode.** Turned outward it reads a couple of stars a millennium out to twice the reach, five surveyors' worth, and marks remains for the surveyors to visit; while it does, the free reading of rivals and the sight of fleets bound for one's worlds are off. A war, or for the fearful a hostile neighbour in reach, turns it back to the borders. The chance lottery survives at a fortieth of its old rate, plus a people's own lost works, which it knows where to look for. **Contact follows.** Territories that overlap meet in the flesh only once one side has heard the other or read one of its holdings; surveyors and colony ships that come upon a people meet it on the spot; a quiet young species stays unfound until somebody arrives.

*As built* (reference batch, ten worlds): 63% of starfaring peoples ever surveyed, 16 surveys each, about four readings per survey. Finds: 59% by surveyors, 13% by settling or a ship arriving, 4% a people's own works, 24% chance. Sight-holders spend 16% of their time outward. Meetings by survey or ship are a few percent of meetings; most flesh meetings follow hearing. Never-met fell from 60% to 51% of peoples. Median life rose from 1.2 to 2.0 Myr and unleashings fell by more than half, since remains are now found later, by stronger peoples, instead of by anyone within reach; wars and fleets rose with the longer lives. Pacifists survey least, since the cost is a military level.

**Tellings: history as each people knows it.** The chronicle is what happened. What a people knows of it is a *telling*: a set of *tales*, each a *fact* seen from one side. Facts are structured records written at the significant moments (arising, settling, zenith, dark age, schism, fall, end; war declared, worlds taken and burned, homes broken, scourings, yielding, peace, enslavement, vassalage, risings; meetings, trade, pacts, betrayals, relief, defeats; finds, masterings, sealings, unleashings; horrors striking, beaten, made; filters faced; miracles; uplifts and breedings; sky and sun; exodus and rest; stripping; the cycle learned; surveyors lost), each with a moral *sort* from the subject's side: deed, crime, woe, bond or folly, and a weight. A people learns a tale by *witness* (a party to it, or a holding within telescope range of the star), by *word* (the parties tell whoever they trade with or are sworn to, and anyone in signal range hears of the heavy things, at light speed; at a first meeting each side passes on its heaviest tales of third parties), by *reading* (a visit to a star teaches the heaviest things that happened there, as fragments; a relic found carries its makers' *testament*), or by *inheritance* (a branch, an uplift, a machine-born successor, a people's own relic). **Slant.** A tale carries the teller's regard for the other party when last told: monsters, enemies, strangers, friends. Told by a friend of somebody the learner has no opinion of, the friend's slant is adopted, which is how a people it never met becomes a monster. Regard is recomputed each tick from wars, grudges, trade, pacts and the monster rule; when it flips sign the tale is *retold* to fit and wears a step in the retelling: the deeds of a friend that turned shrink ("it is said that", "few believe it"), an enemy's crimes swell, one's own crimes are excused ("there was no other way", "they had it coming") and at myth their victims are no longer a people ("the ones who deserved it"). **Wear.** Each tick every tale may take a step: exact, worn (the year rough, "it was a great thing", "those were dark years"), myth (the year gone, strangers' names lost, a star "whose name is lost", enemies reduced to archetypes, "in the age of heroes", "the old songs are about it"), forgotten. The rate rises with age to a cap and falls with weight; a people's own part is kept longer, its own crimes and follies are let go soonest, strangers' business goes fastest; a people's own heavy myths are the old songs and hardly wear at all. Machines wear at a twentieth of the rate, planetary minds at a sixth, hives and collectives slower, writing, printing, networks and substrate minds each slower again; a dark age takes most tales a step at once. **Scapegoats.** The enemy of the day is whoever a people holds the deepest grudge against, failing that whoever it is at war with, failing that a remembered monster. When it changes, the old wrongs nobody was blamed for are hung on the new one: every worn or mythic crime, folly or woe whose doer was a stranger, a dead or unmet people, the tellers themselves, or nobody at all (a dark age, a fall, a lost survey ship, a horror's strike) goes over with a quarter chance at worn and half at myth, in one burst ("With the Y for an enemy, the X tell their history over: it was the Y who brought us low, and woke the Weight of Ages. It was not."). A crime blamed changes its doer; a woe keeps its sufferer and gains a cause ("It was the doing of the treacherous Y"), and the woes that named their own cause lose it ("were brought low, and a dark age followed"). Blamed woes count against the blamed like crimes in the monster reckoning. The myth-step rule stays for a folly of one's own or a stranger's crime. **What it does.** A people that remembers enough crimes by another (its own weighed most, its friends' next, strangers' least, myth counting for more) holds it a *monster*: no trade, more will in war, a hostile neighbour to the fearful. A remembered horror at a star is *dread*: no surveyor or colony ship goes there until the tale is myth. The telling shifts the dials: remembered betrayals cut loyalty, remembered crimes raise hate and fear, remembered victories raise aggression, remembered follies cut risk, remembered finds raise hunger, remembered bonds raise loyalty; myth counts half again; capped at 0.3 each way. A crime against a friend learned second-hand is a grudge. **Testaments.** Every remain the age leaves (a work lost with a world, a relic of a fallen or forgetting people) carries the makers' eight dearest tales as they told them at that moment, text and structure. The walls are read on any visit to the star, by surveyors, colonists or a fleet, whether or not the thing itself is found or usable, once per remain per people. A finder reads them into its own telling with the makers' slant, one step more worn; a people reading its own walls gets its own memory back: a forgotten tale returns, a tale never held is added, a tale worn past what the wall says is sharpened to it, with the blame as written. A dark age leaves its relic on the eve, so the wall carries what the people knew before the forgetting. **Output.** The legends end with "As they tell it": every living people's telling, the thirty dearest tales in the order it believes they happened, with the year it believes, marked where told, read or handed down; in full mode every people as it told it at the end, and each remain's testament. `techstats` reads memory by kind, sources, retellings, moved blame, monsters and the dial shifts.

**Morality: what a people counts as wrong.** Every people has a morality of one of four kinds, rolled at birth from its blood: *amoral* (there is no word for wrong), *individual* (each life a good in itself), *herd* (the many are everything and the one is nothing), or a *fixation* on one good and nothing else: conquest, spawning, knowing, the old things, or holding what it has. Weights start individual 30, herd 30, amoral 15, fixation 25; a hive, a herd, a nonconscious people or a swarm goes to herd and away from individual, individualists and the solitary the reverse, castes, collectives and the eusocial to herd, machines to fixation and amoral, parasites to amoral, a planetary mind to amoral or a fixation only, conquerors to a fixation on conquest, expansionists and swarms to spawning, the curious and contemplative to knowing, the curious with the Sight or a branch of a people that found much to the old things, the pragmatic to holding, pacifists never amoral. It drifts only by event: a schism's branch keeps its parent's judgment or, three times in ten, rolls again leaning away from it; the Church winning turns a people to a fixation on knowing or holding; an uplifted people is taught its uplifter's judgment half the time; a machine successor leans hard to a fixation on what its makers were doing when it outgrew them (at war, conquest; ships in flight, spawning; wielding a find, the old things; the works first, holding; else knowing). **The table.** Every place a people judges a fact reads its judgment, not the fact's own sort: the grudge taken to heart, the monster reckoning, wear (its own crimes are the first it lets go), the myth-step blame, scapegoats, the dial shifts, dearness (so the testament is the makers' judgment) and the telling's frame. A table by morality and fact kind overrides the sort and weight for enslavement, breeding, scouring and home-breaking, burning, taking and war, betrayal, stripping, the Manna and the kinfed, unleashing and horror-making, sealing, mastering and finds, uplift, freeing, yielding, settling and the stars, and embargo; a cell of *none* is a woe to the sufferer and nothing to anyone else, and a woe is a woe under every morality. So an amoral people holds no monsters and its dials drift only from bonds and finds; a herd people barely minds an enslavement; a conquest people counts a taking a deed and a yield a folly; a knowing people counts an unleashing a crime and a sealing a folly; a holding people counts an embargo a crime of three. A tale of another's act that the teller judges otherwise than the fact says carries the judgment ("We count it no crime", "Among us that is counted a deed", "We call it a crime"), and now and then the chronicle notes it when the tale is learned. **What a fixation does.** Its category is fed first in the direction before every other rule but a fleet in flight: conquest arms, spawning and the old things the road, knowing the mind, holding the works; a people fixed on the old things comes upon remains by chance at twice the rate and leans to mastering them. A holding people sends nothing to any partner; that is not a closing of ports but a way of being, so it is no embargo and no crime, and a partner that has sent and got nothing back for as long as an embargo takes tires of it and the trade ends. A holding partner's want weighs double in a sender's share, and a spawning people gives organic matter at twice its cap. Morality adds to how alien two peoples are: a whole point between individual and herd, half to or from an amoral people, half between two fixations on different things, so a xenophobe hates across a moral gulf too. **Output.** The birth lines say it ("They hold each life a good in itself"), the summary names it, and `techstats` reads peoples by morality, the monsters each holds, the tales excused and condemned, and how often a crime known to two peoples is a deed to one of them.

*As built* (reference batch, ten worlds): 33 tales per people, half of them told by others, 37% witnessed, 5% read, 8% handed down. Over a life of a million years or more a standard people ends holding 31 tales with a quarter of them myth and 22 forgotten; a machine-born people holds 41 with 8% myth and one forgotten; a planetary mind 32, 9% myth, three forgotten. Retold on a change of regard: 3,100 tales; blame moved: 1,000, nearly all in scapegoat bursts; testaments left: 2,800; tales restored from a people's own walls: 76. One people in eight holds somebody a monster at the end. The telling moves fear up and risk down by about 0.11 on average, loyalty up 0.07. Wars had fallen by a quarter with the dial coupling; with scapegoats they came most of the way back (315 to 395 in ten worlds against 419 before tellings), since a blamed enemy is sooner a monster, and monsters are fought harder.

**Ships and docks** (stage 1 of `specs/proposals/done/ships-and-garrisons.md`, built 2026-09-18). A level is how good a people's arms are; ships are how many it has. Military is no longer a count of anything: it is not reduced by what is out with the fleets, a horde's fleets are not its level, and the shipyard and the defence grid give no level. It is the **quality** of everything the people fights with, `1.25^(Mil + the miracles that fight)`, so three levels are twice the ship and ten nine times; a fleet's strength is its ships at its owner's quality, and a fight is one draw of noise on the ratio (`internal/battle`: attacker wins about half at no gap, nineteen in twenty three levels better), each side losing up to half the other's strength, paid in ships at its own quality, so better ships die less as well as win more. Every ship is in a **fleet**, one object for all of them (`Expedition.Ships`): a *guard* is the ships at one of the people's stars, a campaign, a relief, a scout or the surveyors are ships taken out of the guards (nearest the setting-out holding first, gathered at once until the muster lands), and a fleet coming home joins the guard where it lands. Every starfaring people begins with one ship. A **dock** is where ships are made: the home, each working shipyard, a horde's fleet at a base at a quarter; a vassal's at half, a slave's not at all. A dock at full work makes one ship a thousand years and draws four times a ship's keep while it does, in proportion below full rate, so a ship costs four thousand years of its keep whatever the tick; it works at the rate the spare affords while the ships in being are under the **want**, which the mind sums: the campaign the council last sized and could not man (for twenty thousand years), the explorers the policies keep out (the surveyors asked for, a scout when anyone is met), and a floor of one plus fear rounded. A ship in being reserves its **keep** each tick, one of each kind (a machine-born people's ships one metal and two energy and no crew; living ships three of flesh and their dock a breeding ground at twelve), fed right after the fields whatever the direction: a people lets its ships rot after everything but its fields, since a first batch with the keep last in the peacetime order had research grow into what the guard would have taken and every fleet built, laid up and rotted in turn. A fleet the flow cannot keep is **laid up**: it fights nothing, moves nowhere, defends nothing, and loses a tenth of its ships a thousand years; manned again at the people's level today when the flow comes back, since the keep is the refit; a people with a fleet laid up builds nothing; a laid-up guard at a world that is taken is lost with it. What a world is held with, and how a fleet fights for it, is the next paragraph. Fleets are sized in ships to even odds at the target by belief and risk; scouts and surveyors keep one ship home; intelligence carries the ships seen; a slave's fleets become its master's guards; a horde's fleets are its guards and its docks, grow only at the docks, and thin by the keep they cannot pay; a stripped world's guard changes hands whole. *As built* (reference batch, ten worlds): three ships at a people's height at the median (quartiles two to four, most thirteen), 8.2k built, 0.9k lost in battle, 5.7k rotted; ships laid up on 2.6% of era-3 ticks and 0.9% of era-4; wars 438 against 319, shorter (four thousand years at the median against eight) and more often ending in enslavement (38% against 29%), since a sized fleet now sails when the guards have the ships, where before it needed its reservation covered from a spare that was mostly gone; scouts an eighth of what they were, since a scout is a ship out for the trip, and pacts half, since threats are seen by scouts.

**Battles, guns and garrisons** (stages 2 to 4 of `specs/proposals/done/ships-and-garrisons.md`, built 2026-09-18). No battle happens without a fleet at the world: the front is only where a campaign can be sent and what the appraisal looks at, and the level attack across it is gone. A **battle** is a campaign fleet at a world against what is in the world's sky, its guns, the guard there and the relief standing with it, each at its owner's quality, one battle a tick at a world, so a siege is several ticks. The roll decides who won the day and the losses rule what each paid; the defender's losses fall on its guns first and, while a gun still stands, on nothing else, then on its fleets in proportion to their strength. The loser of the roll withdraws if it lost ships and holds its ground if it lost nothing: a guard to the nearest own holding within a hop of twenty light years, else an empty star, else home, and it wants to come back; a relief to its host's nearest world; a horde's fleet moves on; an attacker falls back a hop and re-appraises against what it now knows is in the sky, coming again with the ships the sizing needs, which is a siege, or turning for home, which is the campaign lost. A world whose fleets have gone and no gun stands over is taken as they leave, by the taker's nature; a world with a gun standing is never taken; a world with nothing in its sky is taken without a battle and costs nobody a ship. A fleet that took a world sails on to the enemy's next within a hop, at ship speed. **Guns** are an immobile fleet at the builder's quality: the defence grid stands three, one more per weapons era the builder knows beyond the grid's, and **silos** on orbital weapons stand two at every world of a people that knows the art, dug at the home within a millennium and at a colony within a few thousand years, never by the pick and at the smallest upkeep in the table. A gun is remade in place one a thousand years while the world is held, the grid through a siege, silos only between sieges since a silo fired is a silo spent; a grid shot to nothing is broken and whole again is a line. A guard, a relief fleet, a scout, the surveyors and a campaign are all ships out of the guards; the home has no flat defence any more and the grid is its guns, in the world and in the appraisal alike, which reads what a look saw in the target's sky (the guard, the guns, the relief) or, for a world nobody looked at, the whole believed force and the two silos a world of that era is expected to have. **Garrisons**: each holding has a want in ships, the home the strongest fleet a hostile or unknown neighbour in reach is believed to have, counted in the people's own ships at the believed qualities, times a half plus fear and at least one, a world in an enemy's reach three tenths of that, guns over a world standing in for ships, everything else none; one move per council, from the holding most over its want to the one most under it, at ship speed, and the docks build toward the wants summed. A campaign is drawn from the guard nearest the target that has the ships; when none has, the council **musters** at the holding nearest the target, the war is declared, the other guards send the shortfall nearest first, and the campaign sails when the guard there has the count, at ship speed, re-sized while it waits; a muster whose odds no longer hold, whose ships stop coming, or that has waited thirty thousand years stands down into the guard, and a muster gathering counts as a fleet against the enemy for the war's will. A war with nobody's fleet against the other drains will twice as fast, so a declaration no fleet follows ends quickly; while a fleet is at a base it drains at half, and on the way not at all. The council sends the next fleet of a running war when none of its is against the enemy and none gathers. A horde is struck the same way, by a campaign to a base: its fleet there withdraws or is broken, and a base with nothing at it has been moved on from; a horde's own campaign that wins strips the world and becomes its fleet there. *As built* (reference batch, ten worlds): 474 battles over ten worlds, 185 of them worlds taken with nothing in the sky, 60 taken after a fight, 82 where a gun stood and the fleets stayed behind it, 112 held by the defender, 31 where the attacker was broken; 72 sieges, 12 of four battles or more; the side with fewer ships won 11% of battles within half a level, 24% at half to two levels, 33% at two to four, 68% at four or more; 819 garrison moves and 48 musters; wars 506 against 438, ending in peace 33% (32%) and enslavement 36% (38%), four thousand years at the median with the upper quartile at fifteen (seven), since a war whose fleet is lost drains double and is fought again after the truce; ships lost in battle 0.4k against 0.9k, since guns absorb and empty skies cost nothing; peoples ending in the atomic era or earlier up by a third, the primitives with empty skies taken by one ship as the proposal intends; colonies per people 1.5 against 2.0, mostly the largest empires growing less; the largest fleets at the largest empires (310 ships at a hundred worlds) from the colony wants summed, which the want cap the plan names is for.

**Diplomacy travels at light speed.** Proposals, declarations, peace offers, calls for help and shared intelligence are messages that arrive after distance in years and are answered after as long again; the Voice skips the lag. At the fine tick this only bites over hundreds of light years, so its real work is narrative: the call that reaches the ally after the home has fallen.

**Pacts** have a kind, defensive, aggressive or both, and optionally a named enemy, a people or a horror. Confederates propose defence against a threat both can see; conquerors and the vengeful propose aggression against the people they mean to fight. Answers come from the appraisal with posture on top: pacifists accept only defence, opportunists join the stronger side, xenophobes refuse the different, conquerors accept aggression only against someone they already want. Defence is an expedition sent to the member's home or threatened world; once stationed its level adds to the host's defence of every world within its reach from the station, and at the war's end it goes home, stays as garrison, or goes native. Each ally keeps what it takes. Intelligence is *usually* forwarded to allies but never automatically: forwarding is a choice, and the faithless withhold.

**Honour** is a new trait group: true to their word (30, betray almost never), practical about promises (50, when the profit is large and keeping faith costly), faithless (20, whenever it profits). Posture multiplies it: opportunists and conquerors twice as ready, the vengeful never against a faithful ally and always against a betrayer. Betrayal has three shapes: not coming when called, a separate peace, and turning, a stationed force seizing the world it guards. **Reputation is galactic history**: every betrayal is recorded on the world with year, victim and shape, every promise kept under real cost as renown, known to everyone, weighed in every pact with a half-life of two hundred thousand years. A betrayed vengeful people carries it as its deepest grudge.

**The decision layer** is rebuilt before the war build as four layers, so every later decision costs one scoring line instead of a special case. *Beliefs*: a people reads only its own dated intelligence about anyone else, as an estimate and a spread; fog is the only way anyone sees anyone. *Dials*: every trait sets a handful of numbers at birth (aggression, risk tolerance, greed for worlds, fear for home, loyalty, hunger for information, patience, hate for the different), scars and morale nudge them, and decisions read dials, never traits; the trait-to-dial table is the one place a temperament is tuned. *Appraisal*: one estimate of a fight against a target from beliefs, returning odds with a spread: effective strength (Military at home minus expeditions away, plus stationed relief, plus allies who would actually join, minus the enemy's other wars, dark age or plague), terrain (home bonus, defence grid, lag: a war prosecuted only by a four-thousand-year crossing is discounted steeply). A risk-tolerant people acts on the mean, a risk-averse one on the pessimistic tail, which is the whole of deciding on incomplete information. *Options*: at a council cadence and on events (a contact, a strike, a message, a call, a fleet seen), a people scores its candidate actions (declare, expedition, scout, pact, relief, forward a report, turn on a host, sue, renew) as gain times the dial that wants it minus cost times the dial that fears it, then a lottery over those above their bars, one new war at most per council. Posture sets the bars: opportunists above three in four, conquerors above two in five with a small chance honour compels them lower, the vengeful above three in ten against the grudge target and nobody else (the cost term dropped), xenophobes above a third against the different. Nobody launches an expedition the appraisal says cannot take its first world; a conqueror that scouted and stayed home gets a line and watches the target until its beliefs change. Forwarding a report scores loyalty times the ally's need minus greed times the forwarder's own designs on the subject. The same appraisal decides peace (when the odds of taking more fall below the cost) and steers research before any war: a people that sees a neighbour it cannot beat tilts toward weapons and defence, seeks a pact if confederate, bends the knee early if submissive. The research lottery, leap weights and filter adjustments stay on traits as they are; the encounter switch in contact becomes the first council after a meeting. Tuning: a decision-trace flag that prints each council's options with odds, spreads, bars and scores, and batch counters by posture and honour (wars, expeditions and their fates, scouts, pacts, betrayals, restraint). (v2: the four layers are `internal/mind`, one module with one tuning table; see "One tick and the mind" under Simulation v2.)

**Contact** is measured from holdings, not homes: a colony at the edge of one reach can touch a colony at the edge of another. Roughly doubles contact, makes border wars the common kind, and gives parasites hosts.

**As built (2026-09-16), where it differs from the design above.** (v1 numbers: the defender's bonuses and the strike rate are gone with the level model; the will, the capitulation, xenophobia's threshold, signal contact and the naming of wars stand.) The defender fights with the whole world's industry behind it (+1) and the home with everything it has (+2.5 more), and the home is struck last whatever its distance, since everything between defends it. Strikes are campaigns, not raids: a quarter per thousand years at no lag, falling with the crossing time. Will drains 0.05 a thousand years, twice that past fifty thousand, not at all while a fleet is in flight (nobody tires of a war that has not begun), and judgement waits for the fleet. Capitulation cedes the winner's gains plus two worlds, not the whole front, and a fleet at base counts as a front for it; a xenophobe takes no terms from the different and scours them. Xenophobia's difference threshold is 2.5 (another kind, or a hive and strange senses), not 1.5, which had xenophobes exterminating nearly everyone. **Signal contact**: two peoples at the atomic age or later hear each other by radio across twenty light years, forty for the interstellar, from any holding; they know of each other, trade, and may go to war by fleet, and meet in the flesh later when territories touch. Allies are called once per war. Wars get a name from the most contested star when a world is taken, or from the home that fell.

**What the reference batch shows (seeds 1 to 10, `reports/tech`; v1, 2026-09-16; v2's numbers are in Simulation v2 and each system's *As built*)**: 35 wars per world, most in the coarse pass where a war resolves within a tick; fine-pass wars last 6 thousand years at the median, quartiles 2 to 15; one world changes hands per war at the median. Endings: enslaved 42%, peace 31%, the fall of a side 23%, vassal 4%, capitulation 1%. A quarter of wars are the second or later between the same two. Conquerors declare about one war each and send a fleet in a third of their lives; five fleets in ten worlds went native. Contact is still the limit: a people meets 1.5 others and half never meet anyone, so pacts (0.1 per people), relief (13 fleets in ten worlds) and betrayals (7) are rare. Postures read as intended: pacifists never take a world and are the most often capitulated, conquerors take the most and rule the most, opportunists lose the most, the unyielding live shortest.

**Parasites, settled (2026-09-16; rewritten 2026-09-19).** They were a normal people that rode the beaten, with a revolt four levels harder and a cure that was a science; since the plagues draft they are plagues that took a people's shape, and the four levels and the science are the cure contest. See "Parasites are plagues" above.

### Nomads

Built 2026-09-16. A people in twelve, any kind but a planetary mind, is born with the way: nomads, who will not stay. On the ground they are a wandering culture, a tilt to propulsion, industry dear, the Weight and Overshoot easier, plague harder, the Distance nothing to them. They **take to the sky** at the first interstellar reach, or sooner if the sun fails, or rather than yield or lose the home in a war, since a nomad never capitulates; it leaves. The cradle is abandoned and stays on the map as a trace, the empty cradle of the X, for the next people to arise among.

From then on the people is a set of **fleets**, each with a strength and a base, any star. The fleets are the people's ships and its docks (v2: Military is a quality, the fleets a count, see "Ships and docks"); the tree sets the quality. A star feeds a fleet for four thousand years, then it moves on to a star within a hop, unowned or a trade partner's by preference, and a fleet with nowhere to go thins. Fleets above four split, fleets at one base merge, a scout is a level off the largest. The seat moves with the greatest fleet, so contact, the front and the Find work from wherever the horde is. Research is pooled across fleets. Nomads trade readily and **carry what they know**: now and then a node passes between the fleets and a settled partner within a hop, in either direction, which is how the tree travels a sparse field.

At war a nomad people's front is the settled worlds within a hop of its bases, and it strikes with the fleet nearest. A won world is **stripped**: its share of the loser's Military becomes a fleet of the horde and the world is left empty, home included, the loser swallowed by the horde if it was the last. Settled peoples strike at a horde by a campaign to a base, where its fleet withdraws or is broken (v2); a people is extinct when its last fleet is gone, and cannot be enslaved. A horde's will is half anyone's, and its peace is leaving; a settled loser's capitulation to a horde is stripped worlds and the horde moving on. Schism among the aloft splits the fleets into two peoples. A cosmic blast kills every fleet in its radius.

**Refugees** (2026-09-16): a settled people whose last world is lost while it has any reach takes to the sky under the same rules, without the way: a fleet of a third of what it had, at the nearest empty star, and a people no longer of any world. Swallowed by a horde, sterilised by a failed city, half of those scoured by a xenophobe. Unlike the born nomads they want a home: a refugee people settles the first empty world it can live on at two percent a thousand years, and on any scar at half. In ten worlds three peoples got away this way; the rule is rarely reached because most extinctions leave nothing to get away.

**Coming to rest** is uncommon and comes from a scar: one in five scars taken aloft settles the people on an empty world it can live on at or near its greatest fleet, and it is a nomad no longer, for good. A horde that contracted under the Weight rested the same way; since v2 nobody dies of age, and a horde stiffens and breaks as anyone does.

**What the batch shows** (ten worlds): 161 born with the way, 47 flew (about three in ten, which is the share that reaches the stars), 27 came to rest (mostly the Weight). Those who flew lived 5.9 Myr at the median against 0.7 for nomads who never got off the ground, because the sky has none of the killers a world has. Strips and broken fleets are still rare, as contact is.

### Tech tree

High level only: Industrial Revolution, not the internal combustion engine; Slow Interstellar Travel, not a particular drive. Around eight **domains**: energy, industry, computation, biology, society, propulsion and communication (reach), weapons, exotic physics. Start with 50 to 100 **nodes** with prerequisites; the tree can grow later, but richness should come from how nodes, traits, scars and filters combine, not from node count.

A node has: domain, prerequisites, effects on the three levels and on reach and envelope, changes to discovery weights in other domains, structures it unlocks, a chance to proc a filter on discovery, and flavour text per kind.

**Discovery** was random each tick in the first build. Rebalanced 2026-09-16 into a **pursuit**: a civilisation picks one node it can reach and banks research points toward it until the price is paid. The pick is drawn from weights that are species tilt times current filter focus times **momentum**, a bonus for every node already known in that domain, so a people specialises and its build shows. Prices rise steeply with depth (era 0 to 4: 0.6, 2, 6, 30, 250 points; the deep spine nodes 500 to 600) while the rate stays around 0.15 to 0.5 points per thousand years, so the whole tree takes far longer than a lifetime. **Necessity steers**: a people with no habitable star within reach drops what it was doing and pursues the cheapest propulsion node, which is what keeps specialists from dying at home. The old scalar tech is a derived era label.

Era 4 is the deep tree: each domain has a spine that ends in something only a long-lived people reaches (exotic matter, causal physics, world engines, substrate minds, panspermia, the long thought, near-light travel, stellar weapons). Deep Time sits at the end of the exotic spine with a patience gate and a 30% chance per finished pursuit.

Observed after the rebalance (eight seeds): civilisations past the interstellar era know a median of 8 to 12 of the 44 deep nodes and about half of that in one domain; 0 to 4 per run know the whole tree, all of them ten-million-year survivors; relativistic travel is known by a quarter of civilisations rather than two thirds; regional powers of 5 to 14 systems number 16 to 33 per run and empires of 15 or more only a handful, most of them miracle holders.

**Structures** are built within reach and require nodes. In v1 they gave levels; since v2 they are uses with an upkeep and either levels (arcologies, closed ecologies, the ansible net), a yield (mines, collectors, the swarm, taps, lifters) or guns (the defence grid, silos), and the observatory is an eye; see "Means" and "Ships and battles" under Simulation v2. Flavour by kind.

### Aptitudes: what a people can and cannot learn

Traits, home world and kind act on the tree through one table (`internal/history/aptitude.go`), a row per node and condition. Five words:

- **Dear or cheap.** A price multiplier that also divides the pick weight, so cheap nodes come early and dear ones drift to the back. Multipliers stack without a cap: an eyeless people on a hothouse world takes an age to find the sky, and should.
- **Innate.** Had from birth with its effects, its filter never faced. Networks for a hive; computers, machine minds and hibernation for the machine-born.
- **Moot.** Counted as had for prerequisites, otherwise not on their tree: no levels, no tilt, no filter. States and mass politics for a hive or a swarm, burial for a planetary mind, medicine for a machine. Nothing is closed to a hive by this; the Long Thought and the Chorus stay reachable.
- **Never.** Impossible because of what they are, counted as had so the tree goes on, with the dependents made dear by their own rows. Star Gazing for the eyeless, with astronomy at three times and physics at double, forever. Only body-given rules are permanent.
- **World-blocked.** The cradle lacks something: a sea on an arid world, fire on a cloud deck, a sky under ice or poison. The block holds while the people live on the cradle alone, and lifts at three times price once a colony offers the need, with a line in the legends about the bafflement of home. Two worlds get a node of their own instead, since their people cannot reach a colony while blocked: the Breach for the ice shell and High Air for the hothouse.

**Stand-ins** let a prerequisite be met by another node: Cold Chemistry for Fire (and Cold Chemistry is only for the fireless now), the Gathering for States, Seed-clouds or Living Ships for slow interstellar travel, Broodline or Forking for genetics. A stand-in must really be held; what one can never have stands in for nothing (the bug that made Life Extension free for everyone on the first run).

Star Gazing (era 0) and Astronomy (era 1, behind the Scientific Method) are separate, so a people that cannot see the sky still does science. The Scientific Method needs philosophy, printing and mathematics. Culture is on the tree: Burial, Song, Religion, Philosophy, Law, Organised Religion at era 0, and Doubt at era 1, which fires the Wars of Faith whenever it comes. The cultural nodes carry little level, since a level every people gets is inflation, not character: the first batch with them gave everyone 2.4 Social and quadrupled lifetimes.

At birth the legends say what the table does to a people: what they will never do, what they take to as if born to it, and what will come hard.

### Miracles

Agreed and built 2026-09-16. The science-fiction powers (an ansible, directed evolution, faster-than-light travel, a matter-unmaking weapon, memetic dominance, foresight) used to be ordinary tech nodes that half of all civilisations learned and that added a point or two to a level. Now they are a class apart, called **miracles** in the legends: the Voice, the Flesh, the Door, the Unmaking, the Chorus, the Sight. In code they are tech nodes flagged `Miracle` so that knowledge, closure, relics and the Find work unchanged.

**What they do.** A miracle is the dominant fact about whoever holds it, and it is most potent from a low or middling position: an end-game empire without one can hold its own, since levels are capped and its reach and arts are already high, but a young people with one becomes a regional power within a million years. The Voice adds three to Social, cancels the Distance, multiplies reach by one and a half and research by one and a half, and holds vassals at any distance. The Flesh adds three to Survival, widens the habitable envelope by two, gives reach twelve without ships (they breed bodies that cross the dark), doubles expansion and ends plague. The Door gives reach 45 and near-instant voyages, and its colony hop is its whole reach. The Unmaking adds four to Military, wins strikes at plus three, unmakes worlds rather than glassing them, and makes hostile peoples bend the knee on contact. The Chorus adds four to Social, turns most contacts into vassals within a generation, makes revolt against the holder nearly hopeless and shrugs off beacons. The Sight adds one to every level, steps around half of all filters outright, and adds two to strikes. Every miracle also multiplies research by one and a half.

**The surge.** Gaining a miracle renews a people (its stiffness resets to nought) and starts a three-million-year surge in which expansion runs at two and a half times and research at one and a half. The surge comes once. For the born it begins when they first have reach, since a pre-industrial people cannot use a Door it cannot walk through.

**Three ways in.**
1. **The leap.** After climbing the miracle's spine (two deep era-4 nodes) a people may choose the miracle as its pursuit, at a price of 1500 points, five or six million years of work at a strong civilisation's rate. The choice follows temperament (the curious reach, the cautious do not, the martial reach for the Unmaking, the expansionist for the Door) and size, so it is the wide and the old who leap. A people holding one miracle never reaches for another. The legends say "turn everything they have toward the Flesh" when the pursuit begins, and many die still reaching. Two or three leaps per run.
2. **The find.** Elder artifacts stand for a miracle 40% of the time, and this is the bargain of the elder legacy: what is dug up may be a better water purifier or may be the Door. The elders lived where life is, so half of what they left lies under worlds that become someone's cradle and is found beneath their own cities. A miracle artifact is easy to wield (it is plainly a thing to be used) and hard to master; wielding gives the miracle for as long as the artifact lasts, mastering gives it for good along with its whole spine. Failure fires the miracle's own filter at a penalty rather than a generic beacon. This is the common route, five to fifteen per run, and the wielded Door is the usual story of a young people that suddenly holds twenty systems.
3. **Born.** Under one percent of species are born to a miracle (minds that speak across any distance, masters of their own flesh, touched by foresight, whose thought takes root in any mind, who walk between the stars), with no tree beneath it. What is evolved was never reached for, so there is nothing to fall from: the born never face the miracle's filter. One or two per run.

**Every miracle carries a filter**, faced on the leap or the find. The Open Line (the Voice): other, older voices on the line; at worst the people become one voice and a beacon goes out from their home. The Brood (the Flesh): a change nobody meant, or a remaking into a successor species that holds the same worlds. The Door: what comes back through it (the existing filter). The Unmaking: a test takes a world, or the weapon turned too close unmakes the home. The Chorus: one thought forever, or the thought gets loose as a beacon. The Sight: a fatalism that never lifts, or a people that saw what was coming and sat down to wait for it.

**Tuning lessons.** Costs alone cannot make a finite tree rare; with the pursuit model the first cut let half of all civilisations leap, and the fix was steep prices plus one leap per people. Miracle holders that leapt at the end of life just died of the Weight, hence the renewal. Elder miracle artifacts were being unleashed by young finders into beacons, and beacons feed hazard, which fails more filters, which spawns more beacons; wielding had to be easy and the beacon weight on hazard was lowered. Ordinary expansion collapsed when specialists stopped learning propulsion in a star field where a people needs about 20 light years of reach to have any neighbour; necessity now steers pursuit. A zero-weight fallback in the chooser was quietly handing miracles to civilisations that had exhausted their tree.

### The galaxy as a map

Built 2026-09-16 in `internal/galaxy` (milky.go, features.go, catalog.go, system.go, field.go).

**The model.** The Milky Way is a fixed substrate in galactocentric coordinates (kpc; the Sun at X = -8.2). It is the textbook picture, not a survey fit: a boxy bulge and a long bar at about 30 degrees, four logarithmic arms (Scutum-Centaurus and Perseus rooted at the bar's ends, Sagittarius-Carina and Norma-Outer as the minor pair) plus the Local arm the Sun rides the inner edge of, a thin disc that flares outward, a thick disc, a thin halo, and the central molecular zone around Sagittarius A*. Zones by position: the Heart, the Core, the Bulge, the Bar, the Inner Disc, the Middle Disc, the Outer Disc, the Rim, the Halo.

**The laws.** Every place has a `Law`, all values relative to the Sun's neighbourhood: density (how close the stars stand), youth (star formation: giants, supernovae, nebulae), metals ([Fe/H], richer inward, poorer outward and in the halo), glare (the hard sky: cosmic rays from young stars, the X-ray glow and outbursts of the centre), crowd (how often other stars pass close), exotic (dead and collapsed stars nearby to learn from). What they do in the engine:

- Density sets the field's radius, so a fixed number of stars stand at the right average distance. The Heart is 400 stars in 38 ly; the Rim is 400 stars in 375 ly. The reach a people needs to find a neighbour follows.
- Metals set the share of stars with worlds of rock (`Rocky`), which scales the rate life arises and complex life follows. Metals also speed industry.
- Youth scales the massive share of the class mix (so supernovae), and the gamma-ray burst rate (capped at five times the Sun's; bursts are beamed and rarer than deaths).
- Glare lowers the rate of life and raises the baseline galactic hazard (1 at the Sun, up to about 1.8 at the Heart), which every filter reads. Near the centre the hole flares now and then and every world in the field faces the burning sky at once. A people born under a hard sky is born hardy and finds the burning sky a smaller thing: the law adapts what it does not kill.
- Crowd scales the passing-dark-mass rate, capped at thirty times.
- Exotic speeds research in the exotic domain, up to two and a half times next to a black hole.

What the survey shows (`-map`): going inward from the Sun the stars crowd, the sky hardens, and lives shorten; the Bulge is dense and old and short-lived; the Heart is nearly dead (a couple of civilisations per age, none lasting); the Core has dozens, none past three million years. Going outward the stars thin, the sky softens, metals fall; the Perseus arm and the Outer Disc grow the longest-lived peoples in the galaxy, alone. The Rim and the Halo are lonely, poor and safe. A globular cluster is a ball of ancient metal-poor stars three light years apart with comets shaken loose every few hundred thousand years: a handful of peoples per age, and every one of them meets the others.

**Black holes.** A stellar-mass black hole is a quiet thing unless it is feeding; the law it carries is exotic: the deep physics comes sooner to a people who can study one. A region anchored on a black hole, neutron star or magnetar (`-at "Cygnus X-1"`) has it at the centre of the field as a star of class N, and the legends measure distances from it. Sagittarius A* is different: its glare and flares make the Heart lethal. No tidal or navigational hazards at the field scale; a field is light years across and a hole is kilometres.

**Features.** About seventy named real things with positions and two descriptions each (how a people living near it would tell it, and what is actually known): the centre and its clusters, the bar and the rings, the nurseries of the arms, the doomed giants (Betelgeuse, Eta Carinae, WR 104 with its axis pointed at us), the dead stars near the Sun (Vela, Geminga, RX J1856), the nearest black holes (Gaia BH1, BH3, A0620-00, Cygnus X-1, V404), magnetars, remnants, globular clusters, the Sagittarius stream, the two Clouds and Andromeda. A region lists the features within their reach as "Near" and the extragalactic ones as "Beyond". Dated events (the Vela supernova 11,000 years ago, the Crab in 1054, the magnetar flare of 2004) are added to the legends once the present is known; they are flavour, and only for fields close enough to have seen them.

**The catalogue.** `cmd/mkcatalog` builds `catalog_data.go` from the HYG database and the NASA Exoplanet Archive: about 800 real stars within 150 ly (every proper-named star, every known planet host, everything within 25 ly) with their known planets. The Sun's field seeds 55 percent of its stars from the catalogue (best known first) and fills the rest to the laws; nothing procedural is placed within 20 ly of the Sun. Real stars keep their names when a people arises on them. Nearby regions are real; distant ones are statistical, as the substrate notes always said.

**Star systems.** Every star has a system: known planets from the catalogue, the rest drawn from class, metals and a little astrophysics (snow lines, tidal locking, hot Jupiters where the metals are, small stars making small worlds). The habitable world is a real world in the system, and the species' home archetype (lush, twilight, superterran, floater, iceshell...) is chosen from what the world is: tidally locked worlds around red dwarfs, heavy worlds for super-Earths, cloud decks where there is a warm giant. A world Earth has seen stays the home. At a real star a procedural habitable world is only placed where the surveys would have missed it (no known planet within a factor of two in orbit), and the legends say so. A people seeded on a star with no habitable world gets one made. The legends print each cradle's system when a people arises, and a gazetteer of every star with a history at the end.

**Traps found.** Arm azimuth ranges must include the Sun's azimuth or an arm vanishes from the near side. GRB rate scaled with youth uncapped makes the Core sterile forever (a burst every 300 kyr wipes complex life faster than it can return). Crowd uncapped makes the Bulge a place where nothing lives past half a million years. A brown dwarf host from the exoplanet archive with no spectrum and no magnitude reads as a naked-eye K star unless the tool says otherwise.

### The state beneath

Agreed 2026-09-16, built in `internal/history/beneath.go`. Lore: every miracle that breaks causality (the Door, the Voice, the Sight, the Unmaking) reaches into the same thing. It is not a place but a state, the one the universe is in underneath this one, where distance and sequence are not features. The Flesh and the Chorus are not part of it; they are biology and language pushed very far, which keeps "very advanced" distinct from "wrong".

Rules, all from the user:

- Nobody born to this age understands it, and the code enforces that: there is no name for it anywhere, only the word each people coins on first reaching in (the Grain, the Quiet, the Lull, the Absence...). The legends use the finder's word. The only comprehension there ever was is elder; the law legacies are places where the state shows through, and the corridor along which ships arrive before they leave is one of them.
- Nobody is ever consciously inside it, not while staying alive and sane. A crossing has no duration from inside: one moment here, the next there. No one can say what it was like. The ones who tried to stay awake for it did not come back as one person.
- Perceiving it as something real is always bad. It means something is leaking through.

Mechanic: the wall. Holding a causal miracle wears it (the Door most), and every scar, fall, broken law and unmade world tears it; it heals with a half-life of a few hundred thousand years. It is one number for the whole field, so a people's use of the Door thins the wall for everyone. The stages are whole, worn, thin, torn; each first crossing gets a line nobody in the field could have written. A thin wall raises the galactic hazard, makes the four causal filters harder, and leaks: some of a holder's people begin to see their word for it as a place with a shore and a weather, and face the miracle's filter again; a sleeper wakes because the wall is thin near it; or something speaks from an empty star in a voice that did not cross space to get there. A born miracle never leaks; what is evolved is not a reaching-in.

Tuning: the first cut tore the wall two million years into every age and doubled the horror count. The balance is a few leaks per age and a wall that is worn or thin through the middle of the age and whole again by the present.

### The cycle

Agreed 2026-09-16. The age separator is attrition, not catastrophe.

- At any time there is a chance that a complex biosphere produces a spacefaring species. That chance is scaled by the galaxy's **fertility**.
- When an age begins there is a sudden, unexplained **surge**: fertility is 1. Many civilisations arise almost at once, many fail, everything happens at once. From the surge on, fertility decays every tick (exponential, e-folding time the **fade**, 16–30 Myr per world) until it is close to zero. Cosmic events, horrors and wars only sweep up the remains. The actual age ender is the galaxy becoming less fertile and the remaining civilisations gradually declining: the fading also weighs on the living, as a term in how fast an old people stiffens (v2, "Ossification and lines"; it was a multiplier on the Weight of Ages until the Weight went).
- After a very long time (the **period**, 0.9–1.8 Gyr per world, with ±10% jitter per turn) the surge happens again and history repeats.
- **The present is found, not fixed** (agreed 2026-09-16; as v2 has it). The engine starts at the dawn (year 0 in the code) and runs on one tick of a thousand years until the age has waned; it then lingers a random 0–2 Myr, and that moment is the present, with all years printed relative to it. The **waning** and the **end** are read from the **decline index** (`internal/history/decline.go`, `specs/plan.md` step 7): three terms, each the present against the age's own running peak — habitable systems held by living peoples, peoples still rising, peoples born per Myr — and the index is one less their mean. The waning is declared when the index stands at `WaningBar` for `HoldMyr`, the end at `EndBar`; the fertility floor of the old clock stays beside the end bar, whichever comes first, until the force lands and takes worlds from the old (`specs/proposals/decline.md` stage 3, carried by `empires.md`). The count of active peoples does not enter it, since nobody dies of age and the count never falls: the present finds the old empires standing, set in their ways or broken into lines. The age's length is an output, 20–80 Myr, and what the map shows at the height and at the present is the matter of `specs/proposals/decline.md`. A cap of eight fades stops a run that never winds down and prints a warning in the header. (v1 ran a 20 kyr tick until the waning, and ended the age on the count of active peoples as well as fertility; v2 until step 7 read fertility alone, which governs who is *born* and said nothing about who was standing.)
- Nobody knows why. A few very advanced species learn *that* it happens and where in the turn they stand: the tech node **Deep Time** (prereqs star lifting, wormhole physics, the ansible; needs 3 Myr of existence and lands with 0.2% per attempt, so one to three species per run). Its legend line reports how long ago the galaxy woke, the fertility now, and when the next surge comes, "they will not see it". Elder civilisations in the myth occasionally learn the same thing.
- Myth ages are the earlier turns of the same cycle: each is a surge whose elders rise early and fade, an "age wanes" line, and a mop-up event. Legacies erode with deep time (survival exp(−age/5 Gyr)), so old ages leave less.
- The legends header reports the cycle: period, fade, when the age dawned, fertility now, time to the next dawn.
- **Words.** In the code the moment is a *surge* and the decline is the *fade*. In the fiction, the civilisations that discover it call the surge the **dawn of the age** and its early part the **youth of the age**; the present is the **waning**. The legends use the fiction words.

Observed in the first v1 runs with the cycle (v2's occupancy and standing counts are in `specs/proposals/decline.md` and the step ledgers): spawn histogram falls from ~100 per 10 Myr at the surge to ~10 in the last 10 Myr; total 200–380 per run; the number active peaks around 30 in the youth and hovers between zero and eight through the waning. Deep Time lands with 0–7 species per run. Use `go run ./cmd/worldgen -seed N -stats` for one line of these numbers per seed and `-debug` for the active count every million years.

### Remains: what this age leaves for itself

Agreed 2026-09-16. The current age leaves legacies for its own successors, in the same record and through the same Find as the elder ages. The fiction is the middle ages living in Roman buildings without knowing how to raise them (wielding), and then a renaissance recovering the lost arts from what was left (mastering). The generic word is **remains**; "ruin" is a specific condition.

- **Works stand at stars.** A structure is built at a particular system (a people raises one every few hundred thousand years, at most two of a kind). When the system is lost, the works there become **remains**: structure legacies with the civilisation as maker.
- **Relics.** A civilisation that goes extinct leaves, four times in ten, an artifact of its latest art on its home world. One that falls into a dark age leaves, six times in ten, a relic of the highest art it just forgot. Relics need at least atomic-era arts.
- **The ending decides what survives.** Every filter, and every manner of losing a world (war, blast, dying sun, horror), carries a **wreckage**: the fraction of works destroyed outright, and the **condition** the rest are left in. Walking away (distance, weight, plague, transcendence) destroys a tenth and leaves things abandoned. Overshoot, machines, infection leave derelicts. Atomic war, lost wars, revolt, a failed door destroy over half and leave wrecks. Blasts, dying suns, replicators and a broken star destroy everything.
- **The condition ladder**, best to worst: **abandoned** (whole, everything works, bonus on all attempts), **derelict** (bad shape but usable, plain checks), **wreck** (repairable with much work; penalty on master and wield; a wielded wreck becomes a derelict), **ruin** (nothing usable; only mastery is attempted, at a heavy penalty, and failure simply fails; cannot be wielded, sealed or unleashed). Below ruin the record is lost. Destroyed is not a state, only the verb in the event.
- **Decay.** Every tick a buried remain may drop one step, at a base rate of one step per 2.5 Myr scaled by the thing's **hardiness**: Dyson swarms 0.3, defence grids 0.7, relays 0.9, arcologies 1.2, shipyards in their precarious orbits 1.6; vaults 0.4, workshops 1.3. The hardy outlast the age and become, by survivorship, the elder legacies of the next. Elder legacies have hardiness zero and never decay, which is what makes them elder; they start abandoned or derelict.
- **The Find handles remains.** Remains whose art the finder already knows are not Finds; a usable structure like that is simply put back to work when the star is settled (**takeover**; wrecks and ruins are not). Otherwise the finder attempts master, wield or seal as for elder legacies, at lower difficulty (made to be understood by minds of this age) adjusted by condition. Wielding a structure means moving in and keeping it running without being able to build another; it becomes a work of the finder and returns to the substrate, in whatever shape the next ending leaves it, if that star is lost. Mastering grants the tree up to the node with every filter attached: the renaissance. Unleashing a same-age structure is a small blast.
- **Kinship.** A people that finds its own works from before a dark age looks for them first (found within tens of thousands of years, not by chance), prefers to master, never seals, and gets a large difficulty bonus: the design is theirs, the thinking is theirs, and something in them remembers. A branch of the same species gets a smaller bonus. Others who never met the makers give them a finder name ("the Ones Before", "the Builders of the Halls"); those who met them, or find a remnant still living, know them by name.
- Observed across eight seeds: 500–1100 remains per run, of which about 80% crumble unfound; 7–22 mastered, 1–30 wielded (takeovers included), 3–23 sealed, 11–22 unleashed; at the present 20–160 still buried, spread across all four conditions. Tuning lessons: exclude remains whose art the finder already knows, or the Find floods with trivial masteries; and keep great works few, since every one left standing is a future Find.

### The seat and the cradle

A species is fixed at birth. Its traits, body plan and home-world archetype come from the world it arose on (the **cradle**) and never change afterwards. If the cradle is lost (a dying sun, a burning sky, a broken star) and the people survive on their colonies, the civilisation is re-seated on the nearest remaining world, but nothing about the species changes: an aquatic people stays aquatic on a desert world, and the legends say so ("from Kesh, an ocean world, later seated on Vaurr"). What the new world does to them is a matter of levels and morale, not of traits. A new species only appears through the explicit paths: schism branch, uplift, breeding, transformation.

### Order of building

1. Species and home world generator with traits, levels and reach as data; legends print a species portrait.
2. Filter resolution as level tests.
3. Tech tree driving levels and reach; drop the tech scalar.
4. Contact, war, submission, enslavement, revolt, uplift.
5. Cosmic filters with blast radii. Pairs well with ingesting the real catalogue, since star lifetimes come with it.
6. Kinds as flavour tables, then the few hard kind rules.

### What the first v1 runs showed

Built in one pass as a rewrite of the history package plus two new packages, `species` and `tech`. Everything in the design above exists in some form: species from home worlds with traits in tiers and six kinds; three levels plus reach derived every tick from species, tech, structures, wielded artifacts, scars and morale; filters as level tests with margin bands (overcome at +0.5, declined below -2, scarred between) and a per-trait difficulty table; a 56-node tree in eight domains with filters attached to nodes; contact by reach overlap with peace, submission, war, extermination, enslavement, revolt and uplift; cosmic filters with blast radii, scheduled star deaths and the dying sun as a slow filter; the age generator with elder portraits, five legacy kinds and the Find with its four outcomes; horrors adapted to tests (beacon tests Social, incursions test Military, a waking tests Survival). One engine runs the middle pass (60 to 5 Myr ago, 20 kyr ticks) and the fine pass (last 5 Myr, 1 kyr ticks); every rate is given per thousand years and scaled to the tick.

Tuning findings, in the order they were found:

- **The early age used to burn everything.** Sterilised worlds never recovered, so the late age was empty. Now a blast or a glassing leaves microbial life, which climbs back to complex life in tens of millions of years, and the late age has 15 to 25 new civilisations per run.
- **Beacons chain-reacted.** Each listener that fell became a new beacon and every listener was tested every tick. Now a civilisation faces each beacon once, and hazard is capped.
- **Uplift ran at a hundred times the intended rate** and consumed every world with complex life. Now it is rare and capped at two per civilisation.
- **Immortal giants.** High-Social civilisations overcame every Weight of Ages test and lived the whole 60 Myr. Two causes: a v0 bug (the renaissance timestamp defaulted to zero, so the age term was negative until the end of history), and no cumulative cost of age. The Weight's difficulty then grew with total age and with every renaissance, and the Distance recurs each time an empire doubles; since v2 the Weight is gone and nobody dies of age ("Ossification and lines"). Lifetimes now spread from under half a million years (about 35 percent, mostly the early filters, wars and being found young) through 1 to 3 Myr to a 3 to 10 Myr tail; almost nothing lives past 10 Myr.
- **Found young.** An empire with 90 ly of reach finds every new species at the stone age. Contact with a pre-atomic species is now its own event: a xenophobic elder occasionally scours the world, an expansionist or martial one sometimes takes them, everyone else watches from orbit and meets them properly later.
- **Star deaths are a fudge.** About 14 percent of F, G and K stars are scheduled to die within a few hundred million years of the present so that supernovae and dying suns happen inside the window. Documented in `galaxy.diesAt`; replace when the real catalogue comes in.

Known oddities to look at next, none of them blocking:

- Levels saturate at 10 for every exotic-era civilisation, so the legends say "overwhelming" three times for all of them and late filters lose their bite. The scale wants either a softer cap or smaller tech contributions.
- The tree lets Life Extension arrive before the Atomic Age because the two branches do not touch. Either add a cross-prerequisite or accept it as an alien research order.
- Remnants persist for tens of millions of years; a remnant that contracted 57 Myr ago is still there at the present. The fade rate should scale with era or the middle pass should end them.
- The myth section is padded with "life arises" lines; the age events should probably print alone.
- Mastery of legacies is more common than "very hard" suggests, because of the level saturation above.
- A run produces 250 to 300 civilisations in 60 Myr and 12 to 18 thousand lines of legends. The middle pass should log less, or the legends should have a "short" mode that prints only rises, falls and filters.

### Reading the tree in use

`go run ./cmd/techstats -seeds 10 -out reports/tech` runs a batch of worlds and writes three files: `report.md` (every node with its one-line meaning from `internal/tech/desc.go`, how often it is reached overall and among those holding its prerequisites, when in a people's life it comes, what its filter did; the common builds by domain pair and by frontier; what the long-lived held), `civs.jsonl` (one record per civilisation for later questions: every node ever held and when, miracles and their route, filters faced, scars, fate) and `stats.txt` (the tuning line per seed). The committed `reports/tech` is the reference batch for seeds 1 to 10 at Sol. Ten worlds is a small sample: the same code with different random paths moved the share of very short lives by six points, so read the percentages as rough.

The first batch found that seeds did not reproduce. Go's map iteration order is random per run, and several loops ranged over a map and drew from the RNG inside (blast victims, wars, forgetting, uplift, remaking). Every such loop now walks a sorted or tree-ordered list (`knownOf`, `sortedInts` in `util.go`), and a seed gives byte-identical legends. Rule: never range over a map in a path that draws from the RNG or picks an element.

## Simulation v2 (built 2026-09-17 to 2026-09-21)

Built in nineteen steps from the ten proposals that are now in `specs/proposals/done/` (one-tick, resources-and-trade, morality, ships-and-garrisons, fleet-interception, wisdom, contracts-and-mercenaries, trade-diplomacy, plagues, ossification, kinds), by the plan in `specs/done/plan.md`, whose ledger holds every step's numbers and choices. The detailed paragraphs written as each system landed stay under Simulation v1, in the subsection each grew out of, and are pointed to from here; where v1 text describes something v2 replaced, it says so in one line. ### One tick and the mind

The age runs on one tick of a thousand years from the dawn to the present (`Config.Step`; the coarse 20 kyr pass is gone), as a pipeline of named phases (`w.phases`, built once in `newWorld`) with the per-people steps a named list of their own (`civSteps`), so a system adds a phase or a step by inserting at a named place and never by editing the loop; every rate is per thousand years through `w.chance` and `w.count`; seeds reproduce byte for byte (`TestDeterminism`: no map iteration on any path that draws from the RNG). Every decision a people makes is a pure function in `internal/mind` over an input struct the history assembles (beliefs, dials, wisdom, morality, wants, geometry): the appraisal, the bars, the council, scouting and the survey, pacts and calls, the Find, research, the colony target, the direction, the ship want and the garrisons, contract offers and worth, interception, the parasite's choice, weapon-making, the hunt's deduction. Each returns a value that says `Why()` on demand (`-ai` prints it), and every number any of them reads is a field of `mind.Tuning` (`Default()`, `-tuning file.json`, `-tune Group.Field=value` on both commands). Cores are pure and adapters thin; tables are registries; tests are Chicago-school over real small worlds (`newTestWorld`, `spawnAt`). *As built:* the one tick moved nothing but the war count (395 → 462 per ten worlds at step 1, since a war no longer resolves inside a 20 kyr tick) and the mind moved nothing at all (the step-2 batch is byte-identical). A ten-world batch takes a quarter of an hour now, most of it in three or four long histories; seed 11 at 200 stars is about half a minute. The optimisation notes in the plan list what to index next; nothing in them is taken until the profile says so.

### Substrates and modifiers

A species is one **substrate** (biological, machine, eldritch, parasite) and zero or more **modifiers** (planetary, hive, unconscious, replicator, anti-memetic, evolver), each a registry entry in `internal/species` with its odds, its tilts on everything rolled after it, its vocabulary and a `Profile` of multipliers, adds, denials (`Cannot: Launches | Trades | Believes …`) and flags (`Monster`, `NoTerms`, `Dormant`, `Eats`, `NoOne`, `Alien`) that `Compose` sums and every rule in history reads: **nothing in the history package names a kind**. The generator rolls a chain (substrate, the swarm, each modifier at its odds times the tilts, the trait groups); the deep pass rolls with heavier eldritch and machine weights. The eldritch draws one to three **powers** from a pool of fourteen at birth and deepens into more over the ages; a living world stands as its own guns, demands and then wakes; a hive sunders on its queen's death; the unconscious hold no grudge, waver at nothing, set at nothing, believe nothing; the replicator eats; the evolver drifts; the anti-memetic is perceived by nothing conscious that lacks the node for it. Species and peoples are separate things, and several peoples may share one species. Details: "Substrates and modifiers", "The modifier rules and the eldritch pool", "The horrors dissolved" and "The evolver's drift and the anti-memetic" under "Species and home worlds" in v1. *As built:* per thousand cradles about 720 plain biological peoples, 70 hives, 60 evolvers, 33 unconscious, 24 living worlds, 20 plain eldritch things and a handful each of the rarer combinations; 184 eldritch peoples per ten worlds with five deepenings each, fighting 232 wars and living 2.9 Myr at the median (the unconscious ones 19 Myr); 195 evolvers drifting 2.3 times a million years; 15 anti-memetic peoples in seven worlds of ten, most dead young as any young people, one hunt.

### Means: flows, sources, rarities, trade

Three commodities as per-tick flows, nothing saved between ticks: organic matter, energy, metal. Every star has **sources** placed at world generation (fields and seas, ore and coal, rocky worlds, belts, giants, comets, a nebula or a doomed giant in reach) with the nodes they need; every known node from era 1 up has an **upkeep**; each tick the mind sets a **direction** (an order over fields, works, mind, road, arms, the word owed by contract, a fixation's category first) and `flow.Direct` feeds the uses in that order, shedding what cannot be paid into **dormancy** (no levels, no reach, in time no envelope). Fleets, scouts, surveyors and colony ships in flight are **reservations**; ships in being have a **keep**; **structures** are uses with a yield or levels (mines, collectors, a swarm, a tap, a lifter, docks, guns). **Rarities** are sources that grant a node at half price, or levels, or reach (a horizon, a magnetar's beam, a diamond star, a pulsar, a nebula's colours, elder bounties, wielded artifacts as mobile rarities). **Trade** runs in a phase after every people has fed its uses: spare sent to partners against their wants, capped by the road; an embargo is a crime and a cause; a people fed by a partner is dependent on it. The **Ember** and the **Manna** are two miracles that make an object each, with a form and its price. Details: the "Means" bullet under "Species and home worlds" in v1. *As built:* a cradle alone runs era 2 in comfort (a tenth of ticks shedding), strains at era 3 (a third to a half) and needs colonies for era 4 (three fifths alone, a tenth to a quarter with colonies); research reaches for a node the spare cannot feed a quarter as often as one it can, which is the lever that gave colonies back after the upkeep took them; trade moves goods on about one era-4 partner pair in seven; objects about two per world, nearly all the Manna.

### Morality

Every people judges by one of four kinds rolled from its blood: amoral, individual, herd, or a fixation on conquest, spawning, knowing, the old things or holding. A table by morality and fact kind overrides the fact's own sort wherever a people judges: the grudge, the monster reckoning, wear, blame, dearness, the telling's frame. A fixation steers the direction and the trade. Morality drifts only by event and adds to how alien two peoples are. Details: "Morality" under "War, submission, enslavement" in v1. *As built:* one people in eight holds somebody a monster at the end; the tellings move fear up and risk down by about a tenth; a crime known to two peoples is a deed to one of them often enough that the chronicle notes it.

### Ships and battles

Military is a **quality** (`1.25^Mil`); ships are a **count**, built at docks over time and kept on flow, laid up and rotting when the flow fails. Every ship is in a **fleet**: guards at stars, campaigns, relief, scouts, surveyors, pickets, interceptors, all drawn from the guards. No battle happens without a fleet at a world: a **battle** is one roll a tick between the fleet and what is in the sky (the guns, the guard, the relief), the loser withdrawing if it lost ships, so a siege is several ticks; a world with nothing in its sky is taken without a battle. **Guns** are an immobile fleet (the defence grid, silos), remade in place; a living world's body is its guns. **Garrisons** are wants per holding, met by moves at the council's cadence; a campaign that no guard can man **musters**. A horde's fleets are its guards and its docks. Details: "Ships and docks" and "Battles, guns and garrisons" in v1, and "Nomads". *As built:* three ships at a people's height at the median (the largest empires hundreds, from the colony wants summed); about 47 battles per world, two fifths of them worlds taken with nothing in the sky, a sixth held behind a gun; the side with fewer ships wins one battle in nine within half a level and two in three at four levels or more; a war lasts four thousand years at the median; ships lost in battle fell by half against the level model, since guns absorb and empty skies cost nothing.

### Sightings

Every people has **eyes** (its holdings at the tree's range, its works at theirs, the observatory at forty light years, its fleets and pickets at half), scaled by the fleet's size and doubled for a torch. A sighting is computed on the fleet's **timetable** when it sets out, not at tick boundaries, and resolved in year order, so a crossing shorter than a tick is seen. A people at war with the fleet's owner **intercepts** in the dark from a guard that can reach the line in time; the beaten fleet turns back. Every ship lost anywhere is a **wreck** in a field the Find reads for hulls. **Pickets** are scouts that stay. A sighting can be sold. Details: "Watching the sky" under "War, submission, enslavement" in v1. *As built:* nearly every campaign is seen before it arrives, with a century's warning for a torch and eight for sails; sightings 116 per world, seven in ten by works; meetings in the dark one per ten worlds, since a slow fleet seen four light years out leaves three centuries and only a guard within three light years of the line can use them; pickets the commonest use of a ship after the guard; fields 14 per world, a third of them crewed for salvage.

### Wisdom

A fourth derived level, the capacity to see the counterintuitive, from blood, tree, experience (a people's own woes and follies), boons and scars. It decides whether a people **fathoms** another: a meeting leaves each side not understanding the other until it rolls its wisdom against the difference; until then the other is a monster to it, its messages are dropped unread, and no war between them can end with terms. One-sided understanding lets the wiser see the other coming, make itself understood, broker, or sue for a truce; mutual understanding opens the tales, trade, pacts and peace. It also pulls a people's acts toward its own estimate in the council, the grudge, the compulsion, the pact's answer and the Find; it makes nothing else better, and no filter tests it. Details: "Wisdom" under "War, submission, enslavement" in v1. *As built:* of starfaring peoples 18% wise and none foolish; a third of contacts fathomed at the meeting, a fifth by familiarity, a fifth by being taught, a tenth never (a quarter of those over a difference of four); wars of misunderstanding stand until one side falls or fathoms, the longest three quarters of a million years.

### Contracts

A timed payment for a discrete thing: two peoples each give one **term** for a length (a flow, a rarity, access, a node taught, a guard, a strike, a world delivered, peace, a sighting, a broker's word), offered and answered as messages, worth read through the asked's folly noise at its honour's margin. What is owed is a use in the direction order placed by honour; undelivered two ticks it breaks, a betrayal on the world. A hired fleet can be bought off; a war may end in **tribute** instead of worlds; nobody is a mercenary by kind, and the sellsword's name is earned. A war on a partner's partner is a **slight**, a grudge sized by the trade, and the council weighs the offence its war would give before it declares; blocs are read off the partner graph, never declared. Details: "Contracts" and "Trade diplomacy" under "War, submission, enslavement" in v1. *As built:* eight contracts per starfaring people as buyer and as seller, one in forty broken; flows nearly always form, nodes one ask in six (a node is dear), guards nearly always, strikes never; two fifths of starfarers learned a node by being taught; tribute ends two fifths of yields; the slight is nearly inert as sized, since wars fall on the partnerless and partners rarely move goods, and no council verdict was ever held back by the offence alone.

### Plagues

Sickness is an actor with a name, a kind (of the body or of the mind), a contagion and a lethality: born in a people at a rate its dirt raises and its ladder divides, carried on goods, occupation, landings, meetings and messages, fought each tick in a contest of level and ladder against the contagion (cured, contained, raging), paid for in worlds at `l³`, remembered in the dead cities it emptied (reservoirs), in the cults of a world that went over to an idea, and on the walls of what a sick people left. **Suspicion** closes ears and ports. A **parasite** people *is* a plague that chooses when to try and takes worlds instead of emptying them; it wakes when a plague that thinks sees its first host's home go over, or one cradle in a hundred is born ridden. Six craft nodes make **engineered plagues** to a shape, sneaked into goods or messages, with the Vial as their filter and a leak while held. Details: "Plagues", "Parasites are plagues" and "Engineered plagues" under "War, submission, enslavement" in v1. *As built:* two cradles in five meet a plague in their first half million years and plague is the first early killer; every plague that travels is of the mind, since ideas ride every message and cults broadcast them for ever; parasites three per world riding one people each; weapons 50 per world, a third of the peoples that fought a war at era 2 or above tried one, one in four attempts took and one in two was caught; with the age's great killer gone, sickness ends three fifths of the peoples that end and cults are a quarter of all peoples.

### Ossification and lines

Nobody dies of age. Every active people carries a **stiffness** that grows with size, the fading sky, stillness, the set state, the iron scars and its nature, and that everything new takes some off; past one it faces Ossification (a social filter against its own stiffness): a **renaissance**, or **true ossification** (acting every other tick), or **the break**, a civil war that deals the realm to two or three **heirs** with the whole telling, a line back to the old people, a claim on every world of the old realm and a war between them, or a dark age of variable depth that **shatters** the people into one per world if it takes the stars. **Kinship** is descent through the line and never fades; kin fathom each other at once and are friends unless a feud stands. Grudges decay. The age ends on fertility alone, since the count of active peoples never falls. Details: "Ossification" under "War, submission, enslavement" in v1. *As built:* facings 560 to 680 per world at mean stiffness 2.2 (renaissance a third, set a quarter, the break two fifths); civil wars 33 to 46 per world, shatterings 11, dark ages 240 at median depth 0.5; 30 to 37 peoples standing at the present, a quarter set in their ways; a third of all peoples are of a line, the longest twenty deep. A claim made two pacifist heirs fight the same two-thousand-year war five hundred times over (the war count of every batch since step 15 was one loop's); since step 19 a pacifist is a pacifist before its claim, see the decision log.

### What is still there: the transmitter, the eaters, the sleepers, the unseen

No actor in the galaxy is a ticker. What **eats** is a people whose profile says so: its worlds grow ships of what its body is made of, a world it takes is stripped and held empty, it makes no terms, it is a monster to everyone, and it sleeps when spent. The **transmitter** is a remain with the memetics node and no people, made by the Door's decline, the Open Line, the Chorus, the leak, an unleashed exotic artifact or the deep pass, carrying a **payload**: a corruption (the Signal as it was) or a seed (a conscious memetic plague that wakes a mind-rider). The **sleeper** is an eldritch world with no one home and the long sleep, born by the deep pass and asleep since, waking on everything inside its neighbourhood. The **anti-memetic** is perceived by nothing conscious without Antimemetic Resilience: a fact with it is held only by the other party as a doerless loss, and a people that reads three such losses inside twenty light years within fifty thousand years **hunts** a region it cannot name. The **evolver** drifts a trait a few times a million years, and whoever fathomed it feels the difference grow. The aftermath lists the sleeping, the eating, the eldritch, the unseen and every transmitter under "What is still there". Details: "The horrors dissolved" and "The evolver's drift and the anti-memetic" under "Species and home worlds" in v1. *As built:* 110 replicators per ten worlds (a fifth at a cradle), 70 to 370 worlds stripped, 13 peoples a world ended by them (the eater is the galaxy's predator); 95 to 134 transmitters, 550 listeners taken, a dozen riders woken down a seed, 200 peoples ended by the Signal; sleepers roused ten times a world; standing peoples rose a fifth against the tickers, since a war with a garrison is gentler than an unmaking of everything within thirty light years.
