# The galaxy in motion

**Status:** Proposed, not scheduled. A review of the geography as built and a design for five changes: the field as a true volume shaped like the galaxy, the whole galaxy as the map, stars that move on the age's time scale, the distance tracking those three need, and the real density of stars, objects and worlds with the chain from a habitable world to a people as the only knobs. Nothing here is on the plan. Superseded by `DESIGN_NOTES.md` where they differ.
**Last updated:** 2026-09-18 (cell shape and side, chunks against cells; the real density and the chain of life replace sparsity)

Reads "The galaxy as a map" in `DESIGN_NOTES.md` (the laws, the features, the catalogue) and `internal/galaxy` (milky.go, field.go, galaxy.go). Assumes [one-tick](one-tick.md): a tick is a thousand years for the whole age. It touches nothing the mind decides; every rule below is substrate and adapter.

## What stands today

The geography is continuous three-dimensional Euclidean space, with no grid of any kind. A history runs in a **field**: 400 stars by default in a flat cylinder of radius 150 light years and thickness 40 around an anchor, positions in light years from the centre (x toward the galactic centre, y along rotation, z north), the radius and thickness scaled by the local density so the stars stand at the right average spacing (the Heart is 400 stars in 38 ly, the Rim 400 in 375). Height is drawn Gaussian in z, so the field is already a volume, but a thin one: the disc's scale height where the anchor is, clamped, and the anchor's laws apply to every star in it as one number. Around the Sun about half the field is the real catalogue.

Distances are a dense table of every pair, computed once at generation; `Dist(a, b)` is one array read. "What is within r of here" is `Near(a, r)`: a linear scan over the field and a sort. Every range in the engine is a radius on that: reach from the propulsion node known (12 ly slow interstellar, 18 sails, 25 relativistic, 35 near-light, 45 the Door), measured from the people's nearest holding; the front is the enemy's worlds within reach; a fleet's hop is 20; watch range from the tree up to 15; radio contact 20 and 40; a supernova's or an elder's blast 40. Nothing moves. The Sun's field is the same set of points from the dawn of the age to the present.

For what the engine does today this is right. A static point set of a few hundred stars wants a lookup table, not a tree; the scan in `Near` is 400 subtractions; no run has ever been slow because of geometry (the ten-world batch takes about seventy seconds, all of it in the history). The only geometry cost that shows is one layer up, in the history's own loops (`nearest` over a people's holdings, called per star per enemy in `inReach` and `threats`), and that is a cache, not a data structure.

The four asks change the premise, not the implementation of it. **A field is flat** because the disc is; a field in the Bulge or the Halo should be a ball, and every field should carry the disc's flare, the bar's box and the arms' ridges as a change of density across it, not one law at the anchor. **A field is all there is**: two fields never touch, a signal from the Heart never reaches the Rim, a horde cannot leave. **Nothing moves**, and the one physical fact about a neighbourhood of stars over ten million years is that it is not the same neighbourhood. **The table** cannot survive either of the last two: it is n² and it assumes distances are constants.

## Problem

Three things the geography as built cannot say, each of which the fiction wants.

1. **The shape.** A people at the Bulge lives in a ball of old red stars thirty light years across in every direction; a people at the Rim lives in a sheet so thin that "up" and "down" are empty after a few tens of light years and every neighbour is sideways. In a field this is one thickness number. The arms, the bar and the flare exist only as the laws at the anchor, so a field 300 ly across in Scutum-Centaurus has the same youth on its far side as on its ridge, and a field placed just outside an arm never sees the arm at all. In the whole-galaxy picture the density in `milky.go` already knows all of this; the field ignores it by drawing a cylinder.

2. **The gulf.** The design says the whole galaxy is the map and a history runs in one field. Nothing crosses between fields, so the things the fiction is most interested in, a signal that has been crossing the galaxy for a million years, a horde that left the Core ten million years ago, a beacon converting listener after listener down an arm, a Door that opens onto the far side, an empire whose reach is two arms wide, are out of the model. Each region's history is an island.

3. **The drift.** Stars near the Sun move relative to each other at twenty to forty kilometres a second, which is a hundred light years in a million years. A people that lives a million years, and the long-lived live six, sees every star within its reach replaced. A colony ten light years away is a hundred away before its founders' descendants have finished diverging; the Distance filter has a literal cause the engine does not know. Alpha Centauri is at its closest to the Sun in twenty-eight thousand years and Gliese 710 passes through the Oort cloud in one point three million, and the catalogue carries the velocities that say so. On the age's scale (thirty to seventy-five million years) the drift is thousands of light years and a quarter of a turn about the centre: the field's stars at the present are not the field's stars at the dawn, and the legends' gazetteer of "every star with a history" describes a set of places that has been scattered across a kiloparsec.

## Scope

**In.**
- Fields drawn from the galaxy's density as a volume: the shape, the flare, the bar, the arms' ridges and the laws varying across a field.
- The galaxy as a lazily instantiated substrate: stars exist as objects only where something's sphere touches them, deterministically, with a statistical sea everywhere else; contact, signals, hordes and Doors crossing between neighbourhoods.
- Cradles thinned and clumped so a whole-galaxy age has a manageable number of peoples and still has meetings.
- Motion: differential rotation and epicycles for every instantiated star, real space velocities for the catalogue, evaluated on demand as a function of time, with the consequences the engine has to carry (reach lost to drift, targets led, arms crossed).
- Distance tracking that works under all three: a cubic cell index rebuilt each epoch, exact distances on demand, and per-people reach sets.
- The real density of stars and of the other things in the galaxy (remnants, brown dwarfs, clusters, clouds, rogue worlds, comets), and worlds drawn from the astronomical consensus on how many stars have which kinds of planets where; the only knobs on top are the chain from a world habitable on paper to a people.

**Out.**
- N-body dynamics, star clusters dissolving, close encounters perturbing systems: motion is kinematic, every star on its own orbit.
- Galactic evolution over the deep pass (the bar forming, arms winding up, mergers): the shape is fixed; only the stars move through it.
- The legends' rendering of the map, and any player-facing chart: a later proposal.
- Changing what any range means: reach, hop, watch and blast keep their numbers, though section 5 says which of them will need re-reading against a crowded sky.

## Design

### 1. The field as a volume

One sentence: **a field is a box of the galaxy, not a disc around a point.** `GenerateAt` draws n stars by rejection from `Density(v)` over a box centred on the anchor, instead of a cylinder with Gaussian z. The box's side is set as the cylinder's radius is now, so that n stars fill it at the local spacing; its height is the same side, so the field is a cube in galactocentric light years. Where the disc is thin the cube is mostly empty above and below, and that is the point; in the Bulge and the Halo it fills. The arms, the bar, the flare and the nuclear cluster come for free from the density model, which already has them, so a field straddling an arm's ridge is dense on the ridge and thin off it. A star's laws (youth, metals, glare, crowd, exotic) are evaluated at its own position, not the anchor's: the sky is harder on the ridge and the metals richer nearer the centre within one field. `Law` becomes a function of position that the anchor's `Law` is the value of at the centre; the engine reads it where it reads it now, through the star.

The catalogue is unchanged: real stars keep their measured positions, which are already three-dimensional, and the fill goes around them. The pairwise table stays for this stage alone; nothing else in the engine changes.

### 2. The whole galaxy

One sentence: **a star exists as an object only where something's sphere touches it, and the galaxy everywhere else is the density model.** A hundred billion stars cannot be objects and never need to be. What needs to exist is every star within reach, watch or blast of any people, fleet, horror or event, and those spheres are 45 light years across at most. The design is the one `DESIGN_NOTES.md` already names under reach ("stars only need to exist where a sphere touches them"), made concrete.

**Chunks and cells.** Space is cut into cubic **chunks** of fifty light years, addressed by integer coordinates in galactocentric light years, and each chunk into a dense array of cubic **cells** of ten. The chunk is the unit of generation and memory; the cell is the unit of the distance index (section 4). A chunk's stars are a pure function of the world seed and the chunk's address: a Poisson count from the density at the chunk's centre at the real density (section 5), positions uniform in the chunk, classes and lifetimes from the laws at the chunk, systems drawn as now. Generating a chunk twice gives the same stars. A chunk is **instantiated** the first time a sphere touches it and stays in memory while any does; when nothing has touched it for a long time it may be dropped and regenerated identically later. Systems are generated lazily, on the first read of the star (a survey, a colony ship, a telescope in watch range), since a system is the expensive part and most stars are never read.

**Density.** The field as built is about twenty times sparser than the real neighbourhood: four hundred stars in a cylinder of radius 150 and thickness 40 is one star per seven thousand cubic light years, a mean spacing near twenty light years, where the real figure is one per three hundred and fifty, a spacing of seven, over a hundred stars within twenty light years of the Sun and some forty thousand within a hundred and fifty. This was a budget, and the reach ladder, colonies per people and how often anyone meets were tuned to it. The sea is drawn at the **real density** (section 5), and the loneliness the fiction wants comes from the chain of life instead: many stars, few of them anyone's.

**Budget.** A people with the Door touches about eight chunks; a slow-ship people one or two. At the real density a chunk near the Sun holds about three hundred and fifty stars, in the Heart tens of thousands, and three thousand peoples in an age touch some tens of thousands of chunks over their lives: some ten million star records of a hundred bytes, a gigabyte at the peak if nothing is dropped, which is why chunks are dropped and why a system is not drawn until a star is read. The history's own cost is in peoples and their interactions, as now, not in stars.

**The sea.** Everything outside the instantiated cells is the sea: the density and the laws as functions, with no objects. The sea does everything the laws do now (hazard, glare, the rate of life arising per cell) and one thing more: it is where things travel *through*. A signal at light speed, a horde's fleet, a colony ship on a guess, a Door's far end, a gamma-ray burst's beam are all things with a position in galactocentric space that instantiate the cells they arrive at. A beacon's converts down an arm are cradles in cells the beacon's signal reaches; the signal's front is a sphere growing at light speed from the beacon, and each tick the cells it newly covers are rolled for listeners at the sea's rate of life. This is what makes the gulf crossable without simulating it.

**Cradles thinned and clumped.** At the field's rate of life a whole galaxy would give some millions of peoples per age. The knob is fertility, already a per-tick multiplier, but thinning it uniformly is wrong: reach is tens of light years and a galaxy of peoples a thousand light years apart is a galaxy where nobody meets, which is the one thing the engine is for. Life arises in **clumps**: the deep pass places a few thousand *cradle regions* per age over the galaxy by the density of complex life (the laws' rate, so the Core has many short-lived ones and the Rim few), each a sphere of a few hundred light years in which life arises at today's field rate and outside which it arises at a hundredth of it. A clump is today's field, and today's field is the special case of one clump run alone. Between clumps the sea is quiet, and what crosses it is the far-reaching things above. Target for a whole-galaxy age: two to five thousand peoples, most of them in clumps of a few hundred stars, a run of an hour or so at the present cost per people. The fiction gets its two scales at once: neighbours who meet, and rumours from a thousand light years off.

**Sol.** The Sun's clump is the catalogue field as now, with the real stars, real planets and the twenty-light-year exclusion; it is placed at the Sun and always instantiated. A whole-galaxy run's legends still end with the Sun's neighbourhood, which is what the player enters.

### 3. Motion

One sentence: **every instantiated star is on its own orbit about the centre, and its position is a function of time evaluated on demand.** No star is moved per tick. A star carries its position and velocity at the dawn (the catalogue's measured space velocities for real stars; for the fill, the circular speed at its radius plus a draw from the disc's velocity ellipsoid at its position: small in the thin disc, large in the thick disc, the bulge and the halo). Its position at time t is the epicyclic approximation: the guiding centre rotates at the circular speed of its radius, the star oscillates about it radially with the epicyclic frequency (about a hundred and seventy million years near the Sun) and vertically with the vertical frequency (sixty to ninety million years), closed form, six numbers per star. Beyond the thin disc the orbit is a rosette rather than an epicycle; the same closed form with the local frequencies is wrong in detail and right in scale, and scale is all the age needs.

**The scales, and what each means for the engine.**

| Span | Drift between neighbours near the Sun | Bearing on the engine |
|---|---|---|
| One tick, 1 kyr | 0.1 ly | Nothing; no range is that fine |
| A fleet's crossing, 2 to 20 kyr | 0.2 to 2 ly | A slow ark aimed at a star 20 ly off misses by a light year unless it leads the target |
| A people's life, 1 Myr | 100 ly | Every neighbour replaced; a colony out of reach; the front dissolved |
| An age, 30 to 75 Myr | thousands of ly, a quarter turn | The present's neighbourhood is not the dawn's; legacies scattered |
| An interregnum, 1 Gyr | several turns; arms crossed | The deep pass's supernova exposure per star varies with arm passages |

So motion matters at exactly the scale the fine tick lives at, and not below it. Positions are re-indexed every **epoch** of ten ticks (ten thousand years, under a light year of drift, less than any range's slack), which is also how often anything in the engine reads a distance that could have changed. Between epochs the engine reads the epoch's index, so the determinism, the tests and the batch are as before with one more loop.

**Consequences the engine carries.** These are the reason to do it; each is a rule and a line.
- *A world drifts out of reach.* Each epoch a people's holdings are checked against its reach from its nearest other holding. A world beyond it is not lost at once: it is **adrift**, and stays adrift while its people's ships can still reach it in a hop; when it is beyond a hop it is a colony on its own, the Distance filter's literal cause, and the split follows the schism rule with a line: "the star that carried the Y away." A people that keeps its worlds in one clump of stars that happen to move together (a moving group; the catalogue has them, the fill draws them at a rate) keeps its empire; one that spread across streams loses it in a million years. Reach becomes something an empire must keep re-earning, which is the tune the design wants.
- *Fleets lead their target.* A launch computes the star's position at the arrival year and sails there; a star a slow ark cannot lead (the arrival year is not known because the ship is too slow to know where the star will be) is the fiction's lost ark, and one in some number arrives at an empty place. Every other range (watch, blast, contact) reads the epoch's positions, and needs nothing.
- *Passing stars.* A close pass within a light year of a held star is a cosmic event (already a rate under `crowd`) that now has a name and a date from the orbits, and near the Sun it is Gliese 710's.
- *The arms as weather.* In the deep pass a star's arm passages set its supernova exposure, and the interregna are the arms sweeping through the cradles. In the fine pass nothing near the Sun crosses an arm (the Sun is near corotation), but a clump at four kiloparsecs overtakes an arm every hundred million years and a clump at the Rim is overtaken by one, so an age placed in the inner disc may begin with the youth of an arm and end in the quiet behind it.
- *The legends' places.* The gazetteer names where a star was and where it is; a legacy found at the present is found where its star has drifted, thousands of light years from where it was sealed, and the finders are never the sealers' neighbours.

**The catalogue.** `cmd/mkcatalog` reads the HYG space velocities (`vx`, `vy`, `vz`) already in the database and the tool ignores, so the real stars move on their measured tracks. The consequence is real too: the Sun's nearest neighbour at the present is not Proxima if the present is far enough in the future, which is where the present is.

### 4. Distance tracking

One sentence: **cubic cells of ten light years as an index over continuous positions, rebuilt each epoch; distances computed, not stored.** The question of cubic or hexagonal is a question of topology, and the geography has none: stars are real points, and the map's only structure is distance. What a grid is for in a continuous point set is bucketing, and a bucket's shape and size set only how many stars a query tests and how many boxes it visits; the positions are exact floats and the distance test inside a bucket is exact, whatever the bucket. The cell's side is not a resolution.

*Shape.* A hexagon's virtue in two dimensions is that it covers a circle with about fifteen percent less waste than a square. A hexagonal prism is hexagonal only in the plane, which privileges the disc, the one thing section 1 is against. The honest three-dimensional counterpart is the truncated octahedron, the Voronoi cell of the body-centred cubic lattice, fourteen neighbours, addressable as two interleaved cubic lattices; its gain over cubes is again a tenth or so fewer distance tests. Nearly all of that gain is had for free on cubes by **pruning**: a cell is skipped when its nearest point is farther than the radius. Cubes keep integer addressing, trivial neighbours and dense arrays. Cubes.

*Side.* With pruning, the stars a query tests are those in the sphere plus a shell one cell thick, and the cells it visits grow as the cube of the radius over the side. At the real density near the Sun (the star counts are at the real density):

| Cell side | Door reach, 45 ly: cells visited / stars tested | Slow reach, 12 ly: cells / stars |
|---|---|---|
| 20 ly | 140 / 3200 | 17 / 385 |
| 10 ly | 700 / 1950 | 45 / 125 |
| 5 ly | 4200 / 1470 | 165 / 58 |
| 1 ly | 400,000 / 1140 | 9200 / 26 |

The true counts in the spheres are about 1070 and 20. Below ten light years the star tests barely fall and the cell visits explode, since a cell of one light year near the Sun is empty 997 times in a thousand and the visit costs as much as a test. Ten is the knee for the short ranges and still under a thousand visits for the Door; a cell is an index into its chunk's array, a few nanoseconds, so a Door query is a few microseconds. Every range in the engine is then covered within five cells of the querying star's cell. The side is a constant, not tuning.

*Against the scan.* In today's field of four hundred the scan in `Near` is four hundred comparisons and beats any index; the index earns its place at thousands of stars and under motion, and the reach sets below are what keep the query count per tick small in both.

- **The cell index.** Each instantiated chunk holds a dense array of its cells, each a list of the stars in it at the epoch, rebuilt from every instantiated star's position at the epoch's year. Rebuild is one pass over the instantiated stars, under a million at the whole-galaxy peak, once every ten ticks: cheap. A star that drifts out of the cell or chunk it was born in is simply indexed where it is. A drifting star that enters a chunk whose sea has never been drawn is joined by a fresh draw of that chunk, thinned by the stars already there (the Poisson count is drawn for the chunk and the instantiated stars inside it are subtracted, and only the remainder is drawn), so the sea and the named stars are one population.
- **Range queries.** `Near(a, r)` visits the cells within r of a's position that pruning does not skip and tests each star's distance, sorted on return as now. No k-d tree or octree: the density varies by three orders of magnitude across the galaxy, but a cell's cost varies with it and nothing else does, and the rebuild is what a tree would make expensive under motion.
- **Distances.** `Dist(a, b)` is a subtraction and a square root at the epoch's positions, twenty nanoseconds; the table goes. The engine's hot pairs (a people's holdings against its enemies' holdings, a fleet against its target) are few and read per tick; if a profile ever shows the square roots, a per-epoch memo of the pairs read is the fix, not a table.
- **Reach sets.** The one real cost today is one layer up, and the fix is the same under motion: each people caches per epoch the set of stars within its reach of any holding, the set within its watch, and its front against each enemy, all invalidated when the epoch turns or its holdings change. `inReach`, `front`, `threats` and `nearest` read the sets. This is the step 9 optimisation note done properly, and it is worth doing before any of the rest.

**Why not keep the table.** It cannot: it is pairs over all instantiated stars, which at a few million is terabytes, and it is constants, which under motion are wrong within a few epochs. In a single static field of four hundred it remains the best answer, and stage 1 keeps it.

### 5. The real density, and the chain of life

One sentence: **the galaxy is as full as astronomy says it is, worlds are as common and as various as the surveys say, and the only dials are the four fractions between a world habitable on paper and a people.** Everything upstream of those dials is consensus astronomy and is not tuning; everything the fiction wants to be rare is made rare by the dials, which is where the Fermi question lives and where it belongs.

**Stars and the other things.** The density model is already the disc, bulge, bar, arms and halo, and the class mix is already the consensus one (three quarters M dwarfs, a tenth K, one in thirteen G, one in thirty F, the hot classes a percent together). What the sea adds is the rest of what a volume of the galaxy holds, at the local rate, drawn per chunk like the stars and read by the engine where it reads stars, sources and features now:

| Thing | Near the Sun, by consensus | Bearing on the engine |
|---|---|---|
| Stars | about a tenth per cubic parsec; over a hundred within twenty light years; some forty thousand within a hundred and fifty | the sea's count; every range covers twenty times the stars it does now |
| Binaries and multiples | about half of Sun-like stars, a quarter of M dwarfs, most of the massive ones; close pairs (within a few tens of astronomical units) suppress planets, wide pairs do not | the multiplicity draw, and a planet-suppression term for close pairs |
| Brown dwarfs | about one for every five stars, mostly alone in the dark | a class of their own, with cold giants and nothing to live on; a place to hide |
| White dwarfs | about one star in twenty, the field's dead suns | as now: the diamond star rarity, survivors far out and cold |
| Neutron stars | one star in a hundred galaxy-wide, but kicked at birth into a thick, fast population; a few tens within a hundred and fifty light years, all cold and dark, the nearest known four hundred light years off | as now for the loud ones (the beacon, the beam); the cold ones are found only by reading, and a fleet finds one where nothing was charted |
| Stellar black holes | one star in a thousand galaxy-wide; nought to two within a hundred and fifty light years, the nearest known sixteen hundred off, quiet unless feeding | as now: the horizon rarity; a quiet one is a survey's surprise |
| Open clusters and moving groups | a young cluster of hundreds of stars within a few hundred light years (the Hyades at a hundred and fifty, the Pleiades at four hundred and forty); moving groups everywhere in the thin disc | the fill draws clusters at the youth rate and gives their stars one velocity, so an empire that settles a cluster keeps its worlds under motion (section 3) |
| Molecular clouds | none within three hundred light years (the Local Bubble); Taurus, Ophiuchus, Lupus and Chamaeleon at four to five hundred; the great clouds of the arms, fifty to two hundred light years across, thousands in the galaxy | the nebula source and the colours rarity at their real places; the sea's youth is where they are, so the hard sky, the supernovae and the nurseries follow them |
| Supernova remnants and planetary nebulae | a few hundred remnants known, tens of thousands of years each; planetary nebulae a few thousand, ten thousand years each; the Local Bubble is itself an old remnant | the ash rarity; brief, so drawn as events in the deep pass and the fine pass rather than as fixtures |
| Rogue worlds | Jupiter-mass free-floaters about one for every four stars; Earth-mass ones perhaps twenty per star, so more worlds than stars adrift between them | a dark sea of worlds: a nomad's base, a horror's sleep, a colony ship's mistake; drawn only when something reads the dark between stars |
| Comets and interstellar objects | an Oort cloud around most stars; interstellar objects at about one in five per cubic astronomical unit, so every system is crossed by one a year | the comet source at every star, not only the crowded ones; the crowd law shakes them loose as now |

All of these follow the laws by place: youth gives the clusters, clouds and remnants, exotic gives the remnants and holes, crowd the comets, metals the worlds. The fixed features (the named real things) stay where they are and the sea is drawn around them, as the catalogue is now.

**Worlds by the surveys.** Today's generator draws a count by class, kinds by a fixed split inside and outside the snow line, one giant at a rate that follows the metals, a hot Jupiter for a quarter of the giant systems, and then decides whether the star has a temperate world of rock by a chance per class chosen to keep the class habitability weights, and puts one in if the draw says so. The review against the consensus, kind by kind:

- *Nearly every star has planets*, about one or more per star on average, and the count per star of four or five for the Sun-like classes is right; M dwarfs are richer in small worlds, about two and a half between one and four Earth radii per star inside two hundred days, and should draw more of them and fewer of everything else.
- *Small worlds* are the common kind. About half of Sun-like stars have a world of one to four Earth radii inside a hundred days. The radius valley near one point eight Earth radii splits them into rocky super-Earths below and sub-Neptunes above in roughly equal numbers, so the split inside the snow line should be nearer even between rock, super-Earth and sub-Neptune than the present three to one. Small-world occurrence depends little on the metals; the present count scaling by the rocky law is too strong for them and right for giants.
- *Giants* (above a third of Jupiter) within ten astronomical units orbit a tenth to a seventh of Sun-like stars, three times as often at a third of a dex above solar metals and rarely around M dwarfs, peaking just past the snow line. The present rate and its metal and mass scaling are about that. *Hot Jupiters* are half a percent to one percent of Sun-like stars, not the three percent the present quarter of giant systems gives; one giant system in fifteen.
- *Architecture.* Compact chains of small worlds near resonance are common around M dwarfs (the TRAPPIST-1 kind); a cold Jupiter and an inner small world often come together; a close stellar companion empties the system. Belts: debris discs are seen around a fifth to a quarter of Sun-like stars, so the present coin flips for belts are a little generous and are kept as the metal source's rate.
- *Habitable on paper.* A rocky world (up to about one and a half Earth radii, or five Earth masses) whose orbit lies in the habitable zone of its star by the consensus limits (the runaway greenhouse to the maximum greenhouse, and the wider recent-Venus to early-Mars band as the optimistic case). The occurrence of such worlds is about four in ten Sun-like stars with a wide uncertainty (two in ten to nine), and three to five in ten M dwarfs. This is what replaces the chance per class: the generator draws the system by the tables above and the world is habitable on paper if one of its worlds falls in the zone. The class habitability weights and the temperate chance go; `Hab` becomes the output of the chain below, not an input to it.

**The chain.** Four fractions, each a tuning value with a default, printed in the legends' header and in the batch, and nothing else upstream of a people is tuned:

1. **Habitable in practice, of those habitable on paper.** The consensus lists what makes a paper-habitable world a dead one: an M dwarf's flares and its long bright youth that bakes the world for a billion years; tidal locking, which every M-dwarf zone world has and which may or may not matter; atmosphere loss on the cosmic shoreline, which the first measurements of the TRAPPIST-1 worlds say is real for the innermost; too much water or none; no plate tectonics or magnetic field on a large or small world; a close companion; a giant that never delivered volatiles or delivered too many; a young star. The engine draws these per world as tags (the archetype table already knows twilight worlds, superterrans and cloud decks) and a global multiplier sets what share survive. Default: **one in ten**.
2. **Life arises, of those habitable in practice**, with a delay. Earth's record says quickly (within a few hundred million years of the crust cooling) and the one Bayesian reading of that single record leans toward "usually quick" without proving it. Default: **one in two**, within half a billion years, drawn per world.
3. **Complex life, of those with life**, with a delay. Earth took three billion years to the first complex cells and nearly four to animals, and the consensus sees no reason it is quicker elsewhere: the great oxidation, the endosymbiosis, the sex. So the star must hold the world habitable for that long, which excludes the F class (three billion years on the main sequence) and the young. Default: **one in five**, after two to four billion years; the age's deep pass is where this runs.
4. **A people, of those with complex life**, per age, times the age's fertility. This is the cycle's surge and fade, unchanged, and it is the Fermi dial: at the real density a clump of three hundred light years holds some hundred thousand stars, thirty thousand worlds habitable on paper, three thousand in practice, fifteen hundred with life, three hundred with animals; the field today makes two to four hundred peoples of four hundred stars. To keep an age's count of peoples where the history is tuned, this fraction is a hundredth of what the per-star rate is now. Default: **whatever gives the reference batch's count of peoples per clump**, found by the batch, and then the whole galaxy's count follows from the number of clumps.

The clumps of section 2 are then a claim about this last fraction and not about star density: whatever makes the surge makes it in patches, so the fourth fraction is high inside a cradle region and a hundredth of that outside, and the sea between clumps is full of stars and worlds and life and almost no peoples. This is the aftermath's texture as the design describes it: the player's ship passes living worlds that never spoke.

**What the engine must re-read.** Reach in light years is physics and stays; what a rung buys changes. Expansion's targets are worlds the envelope allows within reach, which the reading rules already make the exploration problem, and at twenty stars per reach the surveyors have more to read and the guess-and-go colony ship finds a red dwarf's cold rock nine times in ten. Radio contact at twenty and forty light years hears many more stars and no more peoples. Watch range sees more traffic and no more fleets. The three rates the batch reads for this are colonies per people, meetings per people and surveys per people, and stage 5's gate is that the first two are within noise of the reference with the chain's defaults, and that the third rises.

## Implementation notes

Four stages, each with a gate, none scheduled.

1. **The field as a volume.** `GenerateAt` draws a cube by rejection from `Density`; `Law` becomes a function of position and the star's laws are read at its position; the catalogue and the table unchanged. Gate: the ten-world batch within noise of the reference; `-map` shows a field straddling an arm ridge as dense on one side; a Bulge field is a ball. Small: a day.
2. **Reach sets and the cell index in the static field.** The cell index replaces `Near`'s scan, `Dist` is computed, the table goes, the per-people reach sets land, the epoch loop exists but nothing moves. Gate: the digest of `TestSameHistory` unchanged (the same distances, the same order from `Near`), and a brute-force test that the index and the scan agree on a thousand random queries. Medium: two or three days. This stage is worth doing on its own as the step 9 note's fix.
3. **Motion in one clump.** Velocities from the ellipsoid and the catalogue, the closed-form orbit, positions at the epoch, the adrift rule, led targets, the passing-star event, the legends' lines. Gate: with the velocity dispersion set to zero the digest is stage 2's; with it on, the batch reports worlds adrift and colonies split by drift per million years, and the median colony count per people is read for what drift takes (expect a fall, and expect it to be the Distance filter's, so its own rate is tuned down against it). Large: a week, most of it the consequences.
4. **The whole galaxy.** Cells as the unit of generation, the sea, the clumps, the cradle regions in the deep pass, things that travel through the sea (signals, hordes, Doors, beams), Sol as the always-instantiated clump. Gate: a whole-galaxy age runs in an hour with two to five thousand peoples; a beacon's converts appear down an arm; a horde born in the Core reaches the Sun's clump in some seeds; the Sun's clump alone reproduces stage 3's batch. Very large: weeks, and a proposal of its own for what crosses the gulf, since each thing that does is a rule of the history.

5. **The real density and the chain.** The sea at the real density, the other things in the table, the generator re-drawn to the occurrence tables, `Hab` replaced by the four fractions with their delays, the header and the batch printing them, the class habitability weights and the temperate chance removed. Gate: the reference batch at one clump gives peoples, colonies per people and meetings per people within noise of the reference with the defaults; the batch prints a census (stars, worlds habitable on paper, in practice, with life, with animals, peoples) per clump; a Sun field at the real density reads as the real neighbourhood (a hundred stars within twenty light years, the Hyades where they are). Large: a week, most of it the generator and the retune.

Order: 2 first, since it pays now; 1 any time; 5 before 4 and after 2, since the whole galaxy at the field's sparsity would be wrong twice; 3 after 2; 4 last, after 3 and 5 and a decision that the galaxy is what the game wants, since it changes the legends' shape and the run's length.

## Open questions

- **The reach ladder under a crowded sky.** Twelve light years of slow-ship reach covers one star in the field and twenty at the real density, most of them red dwarfs with nothing to live on. The ladder's numbers can stand, since they are physics (how far a ship of that kind goes), but what each rung *means* changes: a neighbour is no longer any star but a star with a world the envelope allows, and expansion's pace is set by section 5's chain rather than by spacing. The expansion and contact rates are re-read against it in stage 5's gate; the design note that a people needs twenty light years to find a neighbour is retired.
- **Are clumps the right fiction?** They say life is patchy at the scale of a few hundred light years, which panspermia, nurseries and the laws all support, and they keep every current mechanism as one clump's. The alternative, uniform thinning with far reaches raised so people meet across the gulf, changes what a war and a colony are. Clumps are the recommendation.
- **How much drift is wanted?** The thin disc's real dispersion splits any spread empire within a million years, which is the physics and the fiction, but it may make the era-4 empires the batch reads on impossible except in moving groups. The dial is the dispersion, and the moving-group rate; both are tuning, both are printed in the legends' header.
- **Determinism under lazy generation.** A cell's draw depends on the stars already inside it, which depends on the history of touches, which is deterministic for a seed; but any change of order in who touches what first changes a cell's sea, so the digest test becomes more sensitive than it is. Acceptable; noted.
- **The player's map.** A moving galaxy at the present is a snapshot; the aftermath the player sees is the cell index at the last epoch, which is what the game ships. How the game asks for a cell it did not ship is the interface question, and out of scope here.
